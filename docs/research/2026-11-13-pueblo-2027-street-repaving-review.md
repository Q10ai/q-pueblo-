## 3. Historical comparisons and non-equivalence safeguards

The City of Pueblo Public Works 'Featured Projects' page, as inspected 2026-11-13, still lists certain 2026 projects: Elizabeth (City Center to 29th); Jerry Murphy Phase 3 concrete (Glenmore to Montebello); Jerry Murphy/Chinook; among others. KRDO reported October 9, 2026 that Elizabeth work planned for 2026 was delayed into 2027, reportedly due to remaining utility work. The 2026 official webpage and November 2026 reporting use different endpoint descriptions for Elizabeth; reconcile with GIS and engineering scope before asserting identical limits.

KRDO's 2024 coverage identified Dillon Drive north of Elizabeth as a then-planned paving stretch. Mere appearance on both historical and proposed lists does NOT prove the exact same segment was skipped, nor prove funds misapplied. Compare intersection endpoints, resurfacing vs reconstruction, and construction acceptance records. Likewise, do not conflate any 29th Street historical segment with the full Dillon-to-Lowell 2027 proposal unless geometry matches.

Questions include whether utility work, design, appropriation, right-of-way, supplier capacity, weather, and jurisdiction boundaries explain schedule evolution. Document answers neutrally, with alternative explanations and timestamps.

## 4. Funding, governance, dependencies and risks

Primary entity: City of Pueblo Department of Public Works. Potential dependency entities: City Council and Finance, city purchasing and contractors, Pueblo Board of Water Works/utilities, Colorado Department of Transportation, FHWA / Safe Routes to School grant authorities, and PACOG when relevant. Connection is a research lead, not proof of a legal funding obligation.

Preserve the distinction: budget proposal -> enacted appropriation -> lawful transfer -> procurement solicitation -> contract award -> notice to proceed -> paid invoices -> punch list/acceptance -> observed physical condition. Capture separate fund, account, cost center, financing restriction, approval date and receipt for each stage.

Known risks to investigate: premature paving before utility trenching; incomplete curb ramps/ADA; CDOT project interference; spot repairs misdescribed as whole-street resurfacing; project selection fairness; large sections consuming maintenance capacity; omitted resurfacing in underserved neighborhoods; and later pavement cutting. These are risk hypotheses, NOT established violations.
