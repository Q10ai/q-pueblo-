What Q learned

Disasters can interrupt unrelated government services, consume mutual aid, produce insurance and housing gaps and create secondary flood or health risks. Q's shock model separates immediate incident facts, aftershocks, service continuity, deferred work, cost and recovery. It is a public learning model, not incident command.

Federal to individual responsibility

FEMA administers eligible public assistance; NRCS works with eligible local sponsors on watershed protection; SBA provides eligible loans. State emergency and forestry bodies coordinate resources; county commissioners/finance and recovery staff administer local work; sheriff and emergency management communicate incident status. Road and Bridge, utilities, permits, health and human services address different operational needs. Insurers, contractors, nonprofits, schools, residents and volunteers each have distinct roles. These are role starting points, not proof of any particular action or liability.

What did the county report?

The dated county dashboard identifies lodging coordination through 211, United Way, Airbnb.org, Red Cross and local partners; emergency water support through Road and Bridge; six or more county departments contributing shelter staff. Resource requests and funding categories can overlap. Ask who delivered what, on what date, under what authority and at what cost.

Shock absorbers

Financial reserves and grant bridging, mutual aid and backfill, housing alternatives, redundant communications, water and road access, volunteer rest and record retention. Measure restoration time and unserved needs. A second event can hit while capacity is still consumed by recovery. Proposed escalation questions: is there a responsible owner, a validated deadline, an available funding path and a manual fallback?

Your household or organization

Register for official alerts, create a meeting/contact plan, prepare medicines and document copies, plan accessibility and animal needs, and keep a paper copy. Never wait for Q to confirm an evacuation. In immediate danger call 911; official alerts control. Organizations can use the printable continuity checklist and identify staff backfill and emergency contacts.

Accountability and years 1/3/5

2027 establish owner, duty, source, dollar state, outcome and recheck for each project; 2029 test a second-event scenario and verify corrective changes; 2031 evaluate resilience, remaining needs and lifecycle cost. QIFY / GLEAN / SUPRA / EE / FFF / T2Y / Event Horizons and Zero Autopsy separate evidence, transferred burden and lessons. No private addresses, names of survivors or unverified cause claims. A signed obligation is not the same as delivery.

Q processing, forensics and scenario

Trace primary disaster → resource diversion → deferred public work → household/service loss → new financial exposure. Set owner, deadline, funding and manual fallback for each burden. A second event before recovery ends is a scenario, not a forecast.

Builds and usable tools

Proposed public shock ledger with phase, duty, actual action, source, funding stage, outcome, unmet need, owner, review date and correction; separate household consent and official response. Existing preparedness and records checklists work locally. No official incident command.
