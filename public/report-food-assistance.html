Summary

The answer: Pueblo has several ways to get food: free prepared meals at Pueblo Community Soup Kitchen, grocery sacks at Cooperative Care, appointment-based grocery pickup at the By Grace Sunny Side Market, pantry and mobile-market locators, school meals, senior meal and delivery programs, WIC, TEFAP and SNAP. Start with the provider directory below; for an urgent or changing need, call 211 to find alternatives. Confirm schedules before travel.

What it means: Use the network, not one government program. Immediate meals, grocery distribution, children’s meals, senior delivery and recurring benefits serve different needs; a fallback referral matters if a site is closed or unsuitable.

Practical takeaway: Text FOOD to 1-877-644-3663 for TEFAP if applicable; use the named provider directory first for practical choices and 211 if a site cannot serve you.

Useful next steps

  • Text FOOD to 1-877-644-3663 for TEFAP if applicable; use the named provider directory first for practical choices and 211 if a site cannot serve you.
  • For a prepared meal, check Pueblo Community Soup Kitchen at 422 W. 7th St. (published Mon–Sat, 8 a.m.–noon).
  • For groceries, call Cooperative Care at 719-543-7484 or By Grace at 719-470-1004; use the Care and Share/PFP locators for more sites.
  • If the first service cannot help, call 211 for food and related referrals. Ask about transport and accessible pickup.
  • For enrolled D60 students use school meals; for older or homebound people check SRDA and Cooperative Care Nutrition on Wheels.
  • For ongoing benefits use County TEFAP (text FOOD to 1-877-644-3663), SNAP or Everyday Eats as applicable.
  • See named food providers and access details
  • Find other housing and support services

Connections and effects
  • Food access connects transport, household income, housing, school attendance, disability and senior-care resources.
  • Community providers and public assistance complement each other; measure actual service handoffs separately.
Past, present and possible future
  • past: County describes changes to the food intake process effective February 1, 2021.
  • present: November 15 provider directory includes meals, groceries, school nutrition, senior services and benefit routes; no live availability established.
  • future: Eligibility and pickup instructions may change; check the program directly.
Other explanations and contrary evidence

Provider and directory listings can disagree or lag; do not confuse cumulative partner count with current open sites. A referral is not a completed food pickup.

What we still do not know
  • Individual eligibility, live stock, today’s actual hours, transport accommodations, current wait times and provider-specific capacity require direct confirmation.
  • Current complete funding, payments, locally measured outcomes and unduplicated household counts were not reconciled.
  • The directory is not exhaustive; rural, D70, college, faith and informal providers require continued expansion and verification.
