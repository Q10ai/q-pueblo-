Pueblo County pension funding, workforce cuts and future obligations

The 2024 ACFR reports $145.10M net liability for the county officers and employees plan, $423,031 PERA net liability and a $4.215M actuarial contribution shortfall for the county plan. These are long-term obligations, not current General Fund bills. [S1]

Independent educational public-source review, not a certified audit or government finding. Evidence status and proposals are distinguished below; missing information does not prevent publication of available findings.

Findings and processing · Unresolved questions · Original sources · Download structured packets

1. Pension balance sheet

At December 31, 2024, total pension liability $314,240,583 minus fiduciary net position $169,141,289 = net liability $145,099,294; assets are 53.83% of liability. 2023 net liability $133,241,186; increase $11,858,108. [S1 p78]

2. Separate PERA participation

The county reports PERA net liability $423,031 and combined reported net pension liability $145,522,325. This does not establish which individual elected officials are enrolled in either plan. [S1 p89]

3. 2024 cash movement

Employer contributions $8,188,507; employee contributions $6,004,905; benefits paid $19,875,889; investment income $10,823,331. Benefits are not an operating deficit and investment income is not guaranteed. [S1 p78]

4. Contribution history

Actuarially determined versus actual employer contributions (millions, rounded): 2015 6.34/3.83; 2016 6.88/4.06; 2017 7.15/4.17; 2018 7.32/4.31; 2019 8.03/4.72; 2020 9.55/5.13; 2021 9.56/6.14; 2022 10.90/6.88; 2023 11.49/7.82; 2024 12.40/8.19. Shortfalls occur in all ten reported years, across administrations. [S1 p111]

5. Exact current historical shortfall

$12,403,537 − $8,188,507 = $4,215,030 in 2024. The schedule reports a contribution of 11.22% of covered payroll; that ratio is not necessarily the statutory or policy contribution rate. [S1 p111]

6. Discount-rate sensitivity

The actuarial disclosure gives $181,528,875 net liability at 6%, $145,099,294 at 7%, and $114,433,124 at 8%. These are valuation scenarios, not an immediate demand for cash or proof of insolvency. [S1 p78]

7. Layoff crossover

Covered payroll $72,951,948 in 2024. A reduction in payroll can reduce payroll-linked contributions, while accrued obligations do not disappear. Retirement timing, replacement hiring, contributions and asset returns require qualified actuarial modeling. [S1 p111]

8. What workers and retirees need

Clear benefit rules, accurate contributions, accessible aggregate funding data and independent advice. Q should not imply a pension reduction or benefit default or give personal retirement advice.

9. Community signal

Comments express concern about employee safety and fairness and claim commissioners use a different pension scheme. The aggregate ACFR cannot establish those individual enrollments. Keep the claim unresolved instead of amplifying it.

10. Child impact and economic resilience

Possible pathways include lost income, family housing instability and changes to service capacity. Retiree spending and the future tax/service tradeoff are regional concerns; not quantified outcomes.

11. Q processing and EE

Store actuarial measurement date, valuation method, discount rate, total liability, fiduciary assets, plan, contribution target and contributions. Do not conflate pension expense, contribution, debt principal or annual budget deficit. Reuse historic data once with auditable calculations.

12. RX and event horizons

Compare next valuation and ACFR, detect recurring deficiency, review employer-policy changes, model payroll and returns with an actuary. Evaluate year 3 and year 5 funding paths; no cost savings or changes to benefits assumed.

What remains unresolved

  • 2025 and 2026 liabilities and actuarial forecasts not verified.
  • Individual retirement enrollment and current contribution policy not reconciled.
  • Effect of proposed cuts on covered payroll and actuarial outcomes not calculated.
