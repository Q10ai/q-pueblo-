2027 nonprofit funding: requests versus proposed City support

Evidence checked 2026-11-09 · Check the listed review date.

Findings and more detail · Sources

Short answer

The City of Pueblo reports approximately $2.2 million in 2027 nonprofit funding requests from 46 organizations, while its 2027 proposed budget includes $490,000 for CSAC. That proposed allocation remains subject to Council approval. A request, proposed allocation, recommendation and award are different stages.

The full story

A partnership built over two decades

Since 2003 the City and County have used the Community Services Advisory Commission to review nonprofit grant requests. The commission recommends funding; the two governments make their own final decisions. In the 2026 cycle, 39 applicants sought $2,333,697. For 2027, the September 1 City release lists 46 applicants seeking $2,203,865, including 15 first-time CSAC applicants. Seven more applications, but $129,832 less requested overall.

What changed this autumn

The City's November 3 statement says the County announced at its October 29 work session that it would not fund CSAC for 2027. The City also reported $465,500 in unpaid 2026 reimbursement and said it had advanced nonprofit payments. These are City-reported claims; the payment ledger and County's detailed response have not been independently reconciled. Separately, a County sponsorship page notes a pause on financial support requests during budget discussions; that broader notice is not itself a CSAC decision record.

Which services are involved

The 46 published requests add up exactly to $2,203,865. Homeless services account for $545,000, youth programs $440,000, health care $304,500, childcare $260,000 and nutrition $160,000. Together those five categories account for $1,709,500, about 77.6% of requested funding. This describes what organizations sought, not approved budgets or measured need. Open the applicant breakdown below to see every amount.

What it means for Pueblo

The City's proposed $490,000 would equal about 22.2% of all published requests. The arithmetic difference is $1,713,865; that is not a confirmed funding shortfall, because applications are not awards and other funding may exist. The City's statement warns of possible disruption to fourth-quarter 2026 payments if reimbursement remains unresolved. A service interruption could shift burdens to families, providers and government, but no specific cancellation is established.

Where things stand

The September notice anticipated recommendations between December 2026 and January 2027. That timetable may change after the October announcement. This November 14 source check verifies the two City statements and the published applicant amounts, not subsequent awards, the County's actual payments or the legal terms of the partnership. The most useful outcome is continuity of essential services with a transparent account of what was requested, awarded, paid and delivered.

What Q finds

The request total and proposed City allocation describe different stages and different funding scopes. Their difference is a planning signal, not a final service-loss estimate or a finding against either government.

What the available evidence cannot establish

  • The City’s description of County withdrawal and the reimbursement disagreement is the City’s position in a public statement, not a neutral finding of responsibility. A request-to-proposal funding gap alone does not identify which programs qualify.
  • Actual approved 2027 City and County awards, if any, and the results of later deliberations.
  • Independent reconciliation of the County’s position on funding and any reimbursements.
  • Which applicants meet eligibility standards and what service levels a given grant would purchase.

Resource efficiency (EE)

Use one cross-government reconciliation and reusable program-level data model rather than repeated manual comparisons.

From evidence to useful results (T2Y)

Source-backed request and proposal amounts are useful, but the actual award and service outcomes remain undetermined and must not be predicted as facts.

Practical remedies and continuity (RX)

Maintain dated releases and recommendations, publish revised grant decisions and keep a clear correction history.

Risks and scenarios

Conditional stress: delayed reimbursement or reduced grants. Compare cash timing, provider continuity and lawful temporary funding options.
