Full story and context

The City’s April 2026 economic-development cash recap, with data as of May 13, 2026, reports $22,085,740 in Council-approved commitments, $66,641,474 uncommitted and $88,727,214 total funds. These amounts represent the report’s stated accounting categories, not a present-day cash balance.

What happened

Pueblo uses its Half-Cent economic-development fund to support eligible investments. The financial recap with May 13 data divides $88.73 million into $22.09 million of approved commitments and $66.64 million uncommitted. Those are dated accounting categories, not today's bank balance.

What connects

The $7.99 million St Charles public-improvement line is already included in the commitment total. Adding it again would overstate commitments. A commitment might support roads or other infrastructure as well as development; it does not establish a particular company's benefit or actual payment.

Why it matters

Residents ultimately need to know whether investments deliver jobs, wages, sustainable tax revenue and useful infrastructure. Those outcomes require later project and spending records; the May snapshot alone cannot establish them.
