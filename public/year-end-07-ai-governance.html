Is Pueblo ready for AI governance? Start before the 2027 budget is locked

Dated research candidate · November 18, 2026

Findings, attributed reports and Q scenarios are distinct. Not an independent completed audit or a current official status. Recheck 2026-11-25; feature through December 31, then reassess for 2027. Source-level freshness controls. Historical reports remain accessible.

Key evidence and current update

The Attorney General states that SB26-189 and the separate chatbot safety law take effect January 1, 2027. Covered consequential-decision uses require specific documentation and rights controls; not all AI tools or entities are covered. Counsel must evaluate each deployment. Proposed rulemaking must not be mistaken for final rules.

Article

AI readiness means governing systems before buying them. Colorado enacted SB26-189 on May 14, 2026, replacing the 2024 approach with rules for automated decision-making technology used in covered consequential decisions. The attorney general's rulemaking page identifies January 1, 2027 as the general effective date. Confirm signed text, current rules, exemptions and municipal applicability by counsel; do not claim every AI system or agency is covered. Q Pueblo proposes a local governance standard regardless of minimum statutory coverage.

Use cases and boundaries

Start with source-linked budget variance explanations, public-record indexing, accessibility conversion, translation reviewed by humans, duplicate invoice flags and procurement document comparison. No initial production deployment of AI for arrests, sentencing, bail, surveillance identification, benefits eligibility, hiring/layoffs, child welfare, medical decisions or emergency dispatch. Any future consideration requires separate legal, rights, sector-specific, safety and public review.

Governance structure and NIST LL

Use NIST's Govern/Map/Measure/Manage as voluntary framework. Designate executive sponsor, data steward, IT security, finance, procurement, legal, accessibility, employee and resident representation. Elected authority approves spending, accountable staff sign outputs; no vendor or AI acts as authority. Cross-jurisdiction coordination by approved agreement, not merged data ownership.

Inventory and procurement

Register owner, purpose, vendor/version, data class, legal basis, affected population, decision type, interfaces, documentation, testing, retention, human escalation, contract term and off-ramp. Require no training on nonpublic government data without authorization, exportability, security/incident terms, subprocessor list, model change notification, audit evidence, service continuity and termination.

Proposed 2027 fiscal envelope (regional shared planning only; USD)

Minimum: inventory/legal 15k; security/privacy 10k; training 10k; integration pilot 10k; assurance/accessibility 10k; contingency 5k = 60k.

Balanced: inventory/legal 25k; security/privacy 25k; training 20k; integration pilot 35k; assurance/accessibility 25k; contingency 20k = 150k.

Expanded: 45k + 55k + 40k + 90k + 45k + 25k = 300k.

These are scenario allowances for labor, procurement and vendor services combined; not local quotes, appropriations, or verified existing budget. Determine internal labor vs cash, recurring costs, division between city/county and funding legality. No use of restricted funds without authority. Start with existing staff and systems, no new data center.

Cost controls / EE

Track full cost per verified completed task, review minutes, error correction, licenses, hosting, storage, energy and export/migration. 20 staff × 2 hours/week × 48 weeks = 1,920 hours illustrative gross time released; at $40 loaded/hour = $76,800 capacity value before review/overhead, not automatically cash savings. Stop pilot if output quality, security, service quality, accessibility or costs fail pre-agreed tests.

Rights and child impact

Keep a human/phone/in-person alternative, plain-language notice, correction and appeal, accessible formats, data minimization and appropriate retention. No children's personal information in public AI tools; school and child welfare crossovers require separate review. Preserve employee participation and retraining, not automated displacement.

Timeline / T2Y / LEAP

Nov–Dec 2026 inventory and temporary safe-use policy, identify contracts and budget capacity, submit scoped line item for lawful approval. Jan 2027 legal gap review, roles, approved environment. Q1 bounded low-risk pilots; Q2 independent measurement/incident drills; Q3 controlled integration only after acceptance; Q4 public report and 2028 decision. Each stage has an explicit human go/no-go.

Shadow / event horizons / incident handling

Shadow AI, incorrect output, prompt injection, unauthorized disclosure, model drift, procurement lock-in and exclusion of people without internet. Isolate system, revoke access, preserve lawful logs, notify appropriate parties and restore manual workflow on critical failure. Human override, deletion per retention policy and rollback mandatory.

Action

Ask city/county to identify current licenses and AI-enabled features, accountable policy owner, use-case inventory, legal applicability and separate funding proposal; publish public-safe register and outcomes. Recheck state rules monthly and upon changes; budget weekly. Links to all seven companion reports.

Q processing and scenario

Compare $60k readiness, $150k balanced and $300k expanded regional scenarios. The $60k option contains $15k inventory/legal; $10k security; $10k training; $10k pilot; $10k assurance/accessibility; $5k contingency. These are illustrative combined labor/cash estimates, not approved funding. Track reviewed cost per task and actual savings.

Red-team and release gates

Reject untraceable output, unsafe personal data, loss of non-digital access, inability to export or restore and unapproved spending. Compare against manual baseline before authorization.

Tools and builds — actual versus proposed

Proposed AI use-case register: owner, law, vendor/version, data, decision type, risk, procurement, test, human review, appeal, kill switch, rollback, retention and incident owner. First pilots read public records and reconcile low-risk data; no autonomous decisions.

Existing Q workbench · Records planner. Proposed modules require data, funding, testing and authorization.

What you can do next

Ask each local authority to appoint a responsible owner, inventory tools, budget a bounded readiness line, and publish a public-safe register and human appeal process.
