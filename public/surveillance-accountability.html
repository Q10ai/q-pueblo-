Surveillance in Pueblo: what is actually in use, what it costs, and who oversees it

Evidence update, November 19, 2026. Part of the existing license-plate reader investigation, not a new canonical issue or independent audit.

The main finding

The records describe more than one technology arrangement. A September 1, 2026 City publication reports approximately 70 Genetec Cloudrunner cameras. A June 14, 2021 Council background paper separately proposed access to privately purchased Flock recordings, stating that the proposed agreement imposed no new City fiscal obligation. Neither establishes current Flock spending, total surveillance costs or the number of cameras in operation today.

September City publication · June 2021 agenda paper

How the system developed

June 2021: proposed Flock data access

The Council packet describes a proposed data-sharing agreement associated with homeowner-organization cameras. The proposed fiscal terms cannot establish an executed or current contract, later renewals or other Flock purchases.

August 20, 2026: policy controls

Police Directive 26-2 establishes interim requirements for department-controlled plate-reader systems. It restricts use to authorized purposes and establishes access and documentation controls. Written policy is not proof of compliance.

Read the directive

September 2026: Genetec system

The City's publication describes its integrated crime-center system and reports about 70 Cloudrunner cameras. The stated operational benefits are claims by the City and provider, not an independent causal evaluation.

Community cameras

The City describes voluntary registration and separate integration options. Registration alone is not equivalent to granting video access. The program's terms distinguish different arrangements.

What this means for safety, privacy and public money

Potential benefits include case support and missing-person or stolen-vehicle investigations. Potential risks include incorrect matches, unjustified access, data sharing beyond a defined purpose, privacy loss, recurring vendor costs and dependency on a single platform. Reported crime reductions alone cannot isolate the effect of cameras.

Compare the alternatives

Maintain current authorized systems with independently reviewed safeguards; limit use to narrower purposes with outcome tracking; or reconsider procurement and non-camera safety measures. Compare total lifecycle costs, corroborated investigative contributions, false positives, documented policy exceptions and independently assessed privacy effects. No single option is established as best.

Financial accountability

Current executed agreements, fee schedules, renewals, amendments, aggregate payments and funding sources have not been reconciled. Unknown cost is not zero. Separate ownership, data access, procurement, subscriptions, support and shared infrastructure. Do not multiply a camera count by an assumed unit price.

Technical evidence and remaining gaps

Track policy version, authorized role, contractual scope, audit findings, aggregate retention and sharing compliance, service measures and redress. Report aggregate statistics without vehicle identifiers, private camera locations, movement histories or security-sensitive deployment details. Do not imply wrongdoing from missing records.

Candidate evidence: approved contracts and amendments, purchase orders, invoices, aggregate authorized usage and compliance reports, independent safety and privacy assessment.
