Why the Sprouts land deal ended — and what Pueblo West can build next

Pueblo West confirmed on April 27, 2026 that the Iron Rock purchase of five tracts was abandoned. The developer's reason remains unknown. A separate King Soopers land sale was approved April 13. This investigation compares the evidence, potential replacements, regional infrastructure and taxpayer risk.

Independent research; not a government finding or live verification. 40 research perspectives · Fiscal calculator · Records packet · Original sources · Structured packet

What this means

Approval, closing, permitting, construction and opening are different milestones. The canceled transaction is confirmed; a permanent Sprouts exit from the region and claims of government fault are not. Grocery choice and affordability, infrastructure suitability and public cost must be evaluated together.

Research paper: 40 perspectives

1. Abstract

analysis — A failed land transaction is a useful diagnostic of regional development readiness. This case compares public land, water, access, competing retail projects, household needs and fiscal exposure. It establishes withdrawal, not causation or wrongdoing.

Sources: S1, S2, S6, S10

2. Scope and question

analysis — Focus on Tract 367, southwest of US 50 and McCulloch Boulevard, Pueblo West; compare nearby development and Pueblo City reuse opportunities without treating jurisdictions as interchangeable. Ask what evidence would make a replacement investment feasible, affordable and accountable.

Sources: S2

3. Research method

analysis — Dated government and corporate statements outrank secondary meeting summaries and social-media leads. No interviews, on-site survey, current title search or independently certified financial audit was performed. Separate facts, reported leads, illustrative models and recommendations; no causal inference from chronology alone.

4. Confirmed outcome

verified — The district's April 27, 2026 notice states Iron Rock Development II LLC abandoned its proposed purchase of five tracts approved December 8, 2025; no agreement then remained in place. The notice identifies a Sprouts development connection without establishing a corporate opening commitment.

Sources: S1

5. Current-status boundary

unknown — No replacement agreement was identified in this bounded November 19 review. The April statement is not a live title report. A later transaction could exist in records not located here; request current disposition, deeds and board minutes.

Sources: S1

6. Chronology

verified — October 2021 marketing guide; February 2025 property inventory; December 8, 2025 sale authorization; April 27, 2026 public withdrawal disclosure. The exact termination date and elapsed negotiation milestones require the contract and notice.

Sources: S1, S2, S3

7. Commercial parcel footprint

verified — Historical guide: 22.61 acres across 14 parcels, of which 19.21 acres are five B-4 parcels and approximately 3.4 acres are nine R-1 parcels. Commercial IDs: 602301003 (4.23 ac), 602301004 (3.25), 602301005 (3.84), 602314001 (5.30), 602313017 (2.59). These are historical candidate IDs, not confirmed contract exhibits.

Sources: S2

8. Land valuation

unknown — The historical asking figure was $1.5 million, not a verified 2025 negotiated consideration, appraisal, actual proceeds or loss. Request the appraisal date, easements, restrictions, developer obligations and final agreement.

Sources: S2, S3

9. Deal structure and diligence

reported — Secondary meeting coverage indicates off-site improvements, water conditions and a revised inspection-resolution deadline were discussed. Obtain the signed agreement, exhibits, feasibility deadlines, earnest-money disposition and any termination notice before attributing a reason.

Sources: S4, S5

10. Competing hypotheses

unknown — Possible barriers include access, utility cost, water entitlement, market competition, financing, title or developer strategy. None is established as the reason for withdrawal. Test each with contemporaneous written evidence; allow multiple causes and an explicit unknown outcome.

11. Road and traffic readiness

verified — The 2021 guide anticipated access changes and a roundabout concept; its historical traffic figure was 16,000 average daily vehicles. Neither is a current traffic count, accepted design or proof of saturation.

Sources: S2

12. Road responsibility

analysis — Confirm county right-of-way and CDOT state-highway access jurisdiction by segment. Ask for traffic impact analysis, access permits, turning movements, emergency access, maintenance agreements and dated comments. Do not assign blame to Road and Bridge without records.

13. Water readiness

verified — Historical plans describe existing mains and 14 then-allocated taps. The district's current published growth policy caps new tap sales at 200 annually for 2026–2029 and monitors new non-single-family use. A tap count is not a commercial demand entitlement.

Sources: S2, S7

14. Wastewater readiness

verified — The older guide identifies sewer connections near site edges; commercial parcels were listed with sewer availability in the 2025 inventory. Obtain capacity, connection routing, elevation, extension cost and downstream treatment confirmation. Presence is not capacity.

Sources: S2, S3

15. Electricity, gas and communications

analysis — The guide identifies Black Hills Energy electric and Xcel gas infrastructure and possible load-related reinforcement. Seek utility will-serve letters, three-phase capacity, refrigeration peak load, transformer lead times, gas pressure and broadband options; do not publish sensitive technical network details.

Sources: S2, S3

16. Drainage, environment and soil

analysis — Require topographic survey, stormwater plan, flood review, environmental screening and geotechnical investigation. The older guide did not establish a site-specific soil test. A feasibility budget must include remediation contingencies and adjacent impacts.

Sources: S2

17. Incentives and public improvement fees

analysis — The historical guide contemplated incentives and a property-based public improvement fee. Confirm current legal requirements, who collects and benefits, caps, sunset, maintenance and consumer price effects. A privately imposed fee is not the same as public tax revenue.

Sources: S2

18. Market demand and leakage

verified — The guide historically estimated grocery leakage at $24.7 million and overall retail leakage above $151 million. The estimates predate this case and cannot be treated as 2026 demand, tax losses, or a new store's projected sales.

Sources: S2

19. Demographic baseline

verified — Census QuickFacts gives Pueblo West CDP 2022 retail sales of $389.591 million, 2020–24 median household income of $96,810 (2024 dollars), poverty 7.0% and average commute 27.1 minutes. CDP boundaries differ from service and tax jurisdictions.

Sources: S8

20. Community input method

community — Four user-submitted overlapping screenshots show 35 reactions, four shares and 15 distinct visible comment blocks, including partial comments. Deduplicate overlapping blocks; classify multiple mentions per comment; no names or screenshots reproduced. This is a convenience sample, not a poll, voter count or proof of demand.

21. Retail preferences

community — Visible mentions: Trader Joe's 4, ALDI 2, Sprouts 2, Whole Foods 1, Publix 1, Panera 1. King Soopers is mentioned as a separate development. One comment opposes another grocer and another favors more choices. Counts are mentions, not votes; alternative uses and dissent remain visible.

22. Resident infrastructure concerns

community — Comments raise roads, traffic, utility readiness and development cost. One attributes the failure to a road department and another suggests a Dillon project retailer. These remain unsourced leads, not findings. Historical utility listings conflict with the blanket claim that no utilities exist.

Sources: S2, S3

23. King Soopers comparison

verified — April 13, 2026 board authorization covers a separate 22.78-acre northwest-corner sale to KRF Pueblo LLC. Its proposed 100,000-square-foot Marketplace, fuel center and 7,000–10,000 square feet of additional retail are plans. Closing, permits, construction and opening need separate confirmation.

Sources: S6

24. ALDI and specialty recruitment

verified — ALDI's January 2026 plan calls for an initial 50 Colorado stores in Denver and Colorado Springs markets over two years; Pueblo is not announced in that source. Trader Joe's, Whole Foods and Publix are resident suggestions, not interested bidders. Obtain corporate confirmation before naming a proposed tenant.

Sources: S9

25. Dillon North comparison

verified — PURA reports approximately $18.7 million of eligible public improvements under a developer-funded, tax-increment reimbursement approach. Eligibility is not paid reimbursement or debt guaranteed by all taxpayers. This is a distinct City project, not evidence ALDI or Sprouts signed on.

Sources: S10

26. Existing-property reuse

analysis — Inventory candidate storefronts mentioned near 29th Street and Elizabeth Street; ownership, vacancy and availability remain unverified. Compare lease or purchase, loading access, refrigeration retrofit, accessible transit, environmental risks and utility capacity before choosing over greenfield land.

27. Households, children and equity

analysis — Measure a fixed food basket, travel time, car-free access, SNAP/WIC participation where applicable, ADA routes and schedule constraints. A premium grocer may improve choice but not necessarily affordability; low-cost staples and accessible transportation deserve equal weight.

28. Jobs and existing businesses

analysis — Distinguish construction job-years from permanent full-time-equivalent employment, wages, benefits and local hiring. Estimate sales displacement from existing stores and net exports to the region instead of claiming gross sales or jobs as new regional growth.

29. Fiscal baseline and jurisdiction

analysis — Identify each entity's actual retained tax rate by product category, gross sales, exempt shares, displacement, land proceeds, abatement, infrastructure ownership and annual service costs. Do not combine City half-cent funds, County tax or Metro District revenues as one pot.

30. Illustrative fiscal model

model — Define net annual contribution N = gross sales × taxable share × incremental share × retained rate − annual public costs. Upfront exposure I = public capital and incentives − net land proceeds; 10-year NPV = −I + sum of N/(1+d)^t. Negative or zero N has no simple payback. No local rate or cost estimate is implied.

31. Stress-test results

model — Illustrative downside: $15m sales, 40% taxable, 25% incremental, 1% retained, $80k annual costs, $2m exposure gives −$65k annual net. Central: $25m, 50%, 50%, 1%, $60k, $1m gives $2.5k. Upside: $40m, 60%, 75%, 1%, $50k, $500k gives $130k and roughly 3.85-year simple payback. At 4% discount, 10-year NPV about −$2.527m, −$0.980m and +$0.554m. Assumptions are synthetic, not forecasts.

32. Water-demand scenario

model — An illustrative 25,000 gallons/day sustained for 365 days equals about 28 acre-feet/year using 325,851 gallons per acre-foot. This is not store demand or an existing entitlement. Obtain seasonal, peak-day, irrigation and fire-flow estimates and test drought limits.

33. Aquatic center separate ledger

verified — The July 27 district update lists $3,825,386 restricted TABOR funds, $1,048,061 committed marijuana excise tax funds and $1,242,254 capital allocation, subject to change. Sum: $6,115,701, versus the approximately $6.2m headline; difference $84,299. This is a reconciliation question, not evidence of diversion.

Sources: S11

34. Aquatic center delivery and operating costs

verified — The district approved a baseline 10,400-square-foot facility with six 25-yard lanes (July 27). Earlier scope and financing alternatives evolved. Obtain voter authorization, annual restricted-fund ledger, commitments, design contracts, estimates, cash flow and operating pro forma; do not conflate the pool with the retail tract.

Sources: S11, S12, S14

35. Alternatives

analysis — Evaluate A: no-build or defer; B: grocery anchor with smaller shops; C: existing-building retrofit; D: phased neighborhood retail or food hall; E: health/services or recreation; F: mixed-use PUD subject to approval. None is selected or endorsed as a committed project.

36. Decision gates

analysis — Require current title and zoning, documented access and utility capacity, current demand and independent capital estimate, enforceable land terms and risk allocation, public notice and lifecycle affordability. Missing or failed safety/water gates stop approval regardless of market interest; publish evidence and owner for every gate.

37. Q efficiency and truth-to-yield

analysis — Track staff hours per verified project, duplicate-record rate, time from source change to reviewed correction, open high-risk gaps and proportion of claims attached to dated records. Compare manual baseline before claiming efficiency. A retailer name alone produces no measurable public yield.

38. Products and system integration

analysis — Implement a static source-linked development tracker, parcel register, demand/reuse comparison, fiscal calculator and records request checklist. Future grocery basket, infrastructure GIS and public capital ledger require verified inputs, privacy review and human approval; do not advertise them as live data services.

39. Records acquisition and right of reply

analysis — Stage CORA requests by transaction, engineering and separate aquatic finance. Request electronic existing records, advance estimate before fees, lawful redactions and segregable material. Invite District, County, CDOT and developer to clarify; nonresponse does not prove misconduct.

Sources: S13

40. Implementation, limitations and conclusion

analysis — 0–30 days: obtain contract, withdrawal and parcel/title status; 31–60: refresh traffic, utility and market evidence; 61–90: evaluate reuse versus new build and publish costed options. Proposed review date November 26; earlier if material evidence arrives. Maintain corrections, version history and human release gate. The transaction failed; why it failed and what replaces it remain open.

Sources: S1, S13

Public fiscal scenario tool

Illustrative only. Inputs are synthetic; no local tax, sale or cost is assumed verified. Nothing is submitted or stored. A 10-year discounted model uses constant annual net contributions.

Default scenario: annual net $2,500; simple payback 400 years; ten-year NPV approximately −$979,723.
