Where is Pueblo's public money going?

Living research edition · November 19, 2026 · Independent, not a government service.

Seven investigations; source-index findings and research questions, not authenticated transaction outcomes.

1. Research question

How can residents distinguish municipal commitments, actual spending and measured public benefit? This study tracks seven subjects without assuming a listed proposal was adopted.

2. Method

Use official dated records first; distinguish index observations from underlying instruments. Record missing evidence and retain revisions.

3. Decision chronology

The City archive lists the November 9 recording and November 23 session at 5:25 p.m. The latter is upcoming as of November 19.

4. Little Leaf Farms

Research lead: obtain employment and training agreements, land terms, milestones and enforceable remedies. No incentive amount is authenticated here.

5. Employment outcomes

Count sustained full-time-equivalent jobs, wages and retention against contractual definitions; distinguish projections from actual jobs.

6. Training

Measure participants, completion, placement and wage progression; prevent double counting of state and city support.

7. Water

Request expected water consumption, source, capacity and conservation commitments; no demand estimate is established.

8. Energy

Compare peak electrical load, annual consumption and resilience needs; distinguish utility costs from public subsidies.

9. Pueblo Boulevard

Research lead: obtain final road and signal ordinance, project scope, engineering estimates, contributions and actual payment records.

10. Reimbursement

Track eligible costs and reimbursements separately; recovery depends on contractual terms, tax increments and payment priority.

11. TIF

Tax increment financing generally dedicates qualifying incremental tax revenue under applicable arrangements; inspect the specific agreement before modeling actual recovery.

12. Road safety

Measure crashes, pedestrian access, school routes and construction impacts before and after improvements.

13. PURA records

The authority lists November 10 meeting materials; October 26 is the latest minutes entry observed November 19.

14. City Hall lease

Research lead: request executed lease, rent comparables, utility costs, access and termination terms.

15. Governance independence

Assess decision authority, records custody and disclosed conflicts without presuming misconduct.

16. Big R

Research lead: obtain parcel identity, appraisal, sale authorization, restrictions and closing documentation.

17. Land opportunity cost

Compare net sale proceeds with market valuation and alternative public uses; assess ongoing service costs.

18. Airport leases

Research lead: obtain executed leases, insurance, maintenance duties and term dates.

19. Airport lifecycle

Compare rent receipts with maintenance, rehabilitation and risk-adjusted capital costs over each actual lease term.

20. 2027 budget

Request adopted appropriations and revised schedules; distinguish budgeted figures from recorded expenditure.

21. Structural balance

Recurring revenue minus recurring expenditure measures operating headroom; restricted funds are not assumed interchangeable.

22. Reserves

Review legally available balances and reserve policy; one-time transfers are not recurring revenue.

23. Procurement

Request enacted threshold changes, competitive bid rules, exceptions and approval authority.

24. Vendor concentration

Measure contract spending by vendor after reconciling related entities; concentration alone is not evidence of wrongdoing.

25. Election evidence

November 13 unofficial report records 69,917 ballots and 60.17% turnout; certification is not established by this report.

26. Question 2A

November 13 unofficial count: 14,044 yes and 24,683 no; do not infer legal implementation from counts.

27. Question 2B

November 13 unofficial count: 23,671 yes and 15,392 no; effective dates and collections require separate evidence.

28. Tax scenarios

For a hypothetical legally applicable rate r and taxable purchases B, household incremental tax is B × r; exemptions and jurisdiction matter.

29. Family impacts

Track household budgets, access to services, transportation and employment stability; impacts are hypotheses until measured.

30. Children and youth

Track safe crossings, education access, intergenerational debt and environmental exposure; do not collect identifiable child data.

31. Distribution

Evaluate benefits and burdens across income groups and neighborhoods using aggregate public data.

32. First-order effects

Identify immediate financial flows, legal obligations and direct service changes.

33. Second-order effects

Assess possible effects on local suppliers, employment, traffic and service demand.

34. Third-order effects

Assess long-term tax base, asset replacement and future liabilities without presenting projections as facts.

35. Alternatives

Compare no action, phased investment, performance-linked incentives and alternative procurement or lease arrangements.

36. Public records request

Ask the appropriate clerk or authority for final ordinances, recorded votes, executed agreements, exhibits, payment ledgers and amendments.

37. Uncertainty

The November 9 agenda viewer failed; the full November 10 PURA book and underlying agreements have not been authenticated in this study.

38. Living knowledge

This dated report evolves; historical records remain traceable, while current claims require freshness review.

39. Next checkpoint

Recheck November 23 Council records and subsequent election certification, then update fiscal scenarios only when legal inputs are established.

40. Conclusion

Publish source-backed observations independently of pending research. No transaction amount or payment is established by this study.
