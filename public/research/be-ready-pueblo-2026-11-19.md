# Be Ready Pueblo: community preparedness as regional resilience infrastructure

QP-RES-BE-READY-PUEBLO | v1.0.0 | 2026-11-19 | Living desk-research paper and implementation packet

## 1. Abstract

Be Ready Pueblo illustrates student-led disaster education following the Aspen Acres Fire. This descriptive case study pairs reported initiative facts with official recovery and preparedness records, and proposes a sustained, accessible civic preparedness layer. It does not estimate causal disaster-loss reduction or establish a partnership. [S1–S10]

## 2. Research question

Can a one-time resource fair become a durable, low-cost bridge between residents and authorized emergency services? Evaluate access, retention, coverage, cost and continuity rather than attendance alone.

## 3. Scope and evidence method

Desk research checked November 19, 2026. Distinguish official records, reported statements, inaccessible references and Q proposals. S1 and S2 describe the same meeting; they cannot be treated as two independent witnesses. No interviews, audited accounts, field surveys or causal study were conducted.

## 4. What happened

Secondary reporting describes thirteen CSU Pueblo students organizing Be Ready Pueblo after the Aspen Acres Fire; a representative addressed county commissioners on November 10. The initiative is not a government emergency command function. [S1,S2]

## 5. Event timeline

December 5, 2026, 1–5 p.m. is the reported fair window. Venue, specific room, accessibility and any schedule changes require confirmation from organizers; no confirmed address is published here. Planned classes, kits and an online directory are not yet delivered outcomes. [S1,S2]

## 6. Resource mobilization

The organizers reportedly began without a cash budget and obtained community backing, with more than thirty organizations expected. Zero initial cash is not zero economic cost; inventory donated supplies, venue time, student labor and staff support separately. [S2]

## 7. Aspen Acres baseline

The county dashboard identifies $321,833.63 in direct costs in the September 8 financial report, not total losses. It records 453 households served with temporary lodging and 81 state resource requests. These are historical counts, not today's displacement or incident status. [S3]

## 8. Geographic distribution

The lodging table attributes 196 households to Rye, 153 to Colorado City, 79 to Beulah and 25 to Pueblo; those figures sum to 453. Use the distribution to guide outreach questions, not to identify affected individuals. [S3]

## 9. Financial reconciliation

The county lists a $500,000 grant request subject to agreement and invoicing, as well as state incident costs of $706,815.24. Do not add requested grants, agency totals and county expenditures into a single loss number without overlap and accounting-period reconciliation. [S3]

## 10. Recovery status

The October 19 rebuilding-resource revision describes debris and infrastructure assistance and limits on other federal assistance; verify current appeal and eligibility directly before relying on it. No household eligibility or award is inferred. [S4,S10]

## 11. Authority and decision rights

Emergency management coordinates authorized response. Q Pueblo can explain and link to the agency, but cannot issue evacuation, reentry, shelter or utility safety orders. Call 911 for immediate danger. [S5,S6]

## 12. Warning systems

Register directly for local emergency notifications, keep device alerts enabled and retain a backup battery radio. Do not submit an address to Q Pueblo. The site contains links, not replicated live alerts. [S7]

## 13. Wildfire

Make transport and communication plans before fire season, keep essential items ready and follow official evacuation instructions. Do not enter closed areas or assume historic maps reflect current conditions. [S5,S8]

## 14. Post-fire flood

Burn scars can compound runoff risk; households should follow weather and official warnings, avoid flooded roads and obtain current road and water advisories. Do not publish automated evacuation routes. [S5,S12]

## 15. Weather and outage

Plan for loss of heat, refrigeration, water and communications. Establish backup arrangements for medical devices with clinicians and suppliers. Never use generators indoors or in enclosed spaces; follow manufacturer and official safety instructions.

## 16. Industrial incidents

Use official protective-action instructions for the particular release. Sheltering and evacuation are not interchangeable. Q Pueblo does not calculate live plume exposure or recommend incident-specific protective action. [S6,S8]

## 17. Basic household supplies

Start with the local seventy-two-hour guidance: food, drinking water, lighting, communications, first aid, hygiene and personal needs. Rotate supplies and build incrementally where money or storage is limited. [S8]

## 18. Water model

Baseline US gallons W = people × days, at least three days. Four people for three days require twelve gallons before additional needs. Heat, illness, pets and other circumstances may increase water requirements; use CDC and local water guidance. [S9]

## 19. Medication and disability access

Work with care teams and equipment vendors on safe continuity. Identify accessible transport, communication formats, backup power and caregivers. Avoid collecting diagnoses or individual dependency lists in a public system.

## 20. Children, elders and animals

Create age-appropriate plans, reunification arrangements and pet or livestock transport options. Confirm animal accommodation with shelters before an emergency; never assume admission or capacity. [S6,S8]

## 21. Rural and low-income access

Use printed multilingual checklists, libraries, neighborhood organizations and distributed training as proposed channels. Provide kit-building options that do not depend on a smartphone, vehicle, paid subscription or large upfront purchase.

## 22. Household action plan

Write down emergency contacts, alternate contact methods, meeting points, transport options and backup caregivers privately; practice and revisit after a move. Never store exact addresses or medical details in the public website.

## 23. Business continuity

Proposed worksheet: critical functions, maximum tolerable downtime, backups, suppliers, staff check-in, alternate workspace and insurance contacts. Test restoration from backups and record gaps without publishing company vulnerabilities.

## 24. Housing and insurance

Maintain private property documentation and contact the insurer, landlord or appropriate agency after loss. Follow official cleanup and permitting guidance; avoid entering damaged structures until authorized. [S4,S10]

## 25. Resource directory

Each entry needs owner, service scope, geography, eligibility, language/accessibility, contact channel, checked date and expiry. If expired, retain the link as unverified and remove claims of availability. No live shelter capacity is asserted.

## 26. Institution and entity map

Candidate roles: CSU Pueblo organizers, County OEM/Sheriff official guidance, county building division recovery, libraries and schools outreach, healthcare providers continuity, nonprofits and businesses resource support. Roles beyond documented mandates are proposed, not agreements.

## 27. Alternative A: annual fair

Lower administrative burden but limited reach between events and potential loss of knowledge after graduation. Track repeat attendance, materials distributed and access to a permanent resource list.

## 28. Alternative B: distributed training

Quarterly sessions in city and rural locations could reach more households and reinforce retention. Requires scheduling, venue support, accessible material and trained facilitators; no current funding or commitments verified.

## 29. Alternative C: persistent hybrid hub

Combine a static source-linked directory, printable plans, training and a periodic event. Favor this conditional approach because updates can be modular and public use remains free; no automated emergency operations are needed.

## 30. Illustrative budget

Planning scenario, not a quote: 100 kits × $35 = $3,500; print 500 sheets × $0.20 = $100; training/access support $800; transport $400; maintenance $1,200; contingency 10% of $6,000 = $600; total $6,600. Donated labor and venue use are excluded from cash cost and must be recorded separately.

## 31. Economic model

Let annual program cost C, event probability p and avoided conditional loss L. Monetary break-even requires p×L≥C, ignoring other benefits and uncertainty. At illustrative C=$6,600 and p=0.10, L=$66,000; neither p nor L is estimated for Pueblo. Do not portray the scenario as ROI evidence.

## 32. Shock absorption

Model household liquidity buffer as available cash minus essential outlays and uninsured costs during interruption; stress-test income loss and temporary lodging costs. This is a planning worksheet, not credit or insurance advice.

## 33. Measurement framework

Measure aggregate reach, baseline/post-session knowledge using the same questions, voluntary 30/90-day follow-up, completed plans and kit access. Report denominators and nonresponse; avoid collecting addresses, diagnoses or identifiable hardship data.

## 34. Evaluation design

Compare pre/post knowledge and later retention, not disaster deaths or losses alone. Selection bias and exposure differences confound attribution. A matched or phased rollout could improve inference only with consent, ethical review where needed and adequate sample sizes.

## 35. Implementation sequencing

Before fair: verify event logistics and direct links. After fair: request aggregate participation, inventory and feedback. In January 2027: nominate a directory steward and confirm review cadence; quarterly: run accessibility and link tests.

## 36. Governance and funding

Any institutional funding requires documented approval, procurement where applicable, stewardship and reporting. Q Pueblo remains independent; no partner endorsement, contract or budget authorization is claimed.

## 37. Safety and data governance

Static site, no account, no telemetry in tools, no persistence of checklist inputs, no public household registry. Official alerts supersede all Q guidance; corrections route to existing site contact. Roll back via repository revert after error.

## 38. Failure and stress scenarios

Assume loss of connectivity, stale links, misleading event details, inaccessible materials, duplicate cost entries and volunteer turnover. Mitigate with print copies, review dates, source status, cost scope and named institutional ownership after consent.

## 39. Integration and maintenance

Use stable ID QP-RES-BE-READY-PUEBLO, public route be-ready-pueblo.html, source ledger and related safety, fiscal, housing and education links. Recheck event details by Nov 25 and before Dec 5; archive event as historical after date, preserve versions and continue serving available evidence.

## 40. Conclusion and limitations

A recurring preparedness network warrants evaluation, not a claim of proven effectiveness. The supplied news article was inaccessible; venue, final partners, inventory, attendance, costs and outcomes remain unresolved. Publish as a living desk-research edition, not peer-reviewed scholarship or an official emergency service.
