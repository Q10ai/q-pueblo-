How does Pueblo really attract jobs?

Economic development, taxpayer accountability and public value · November 19, 2026 · Living investigation v0.2.0

This independent research distinguishes promotional activity, signed commitments, actual payments, verified employment and sustained net public benefit. It is not an audit or a finding of misconduct.

The 2025 ballot measure extended the existing half-cent tax through 2031. Ordinance 11125 expands eligibility for catalyst, leakage prevention and placemaking projects, subject to a collective 15% annual cap based on January 1 uncommitted funds. The preliminary December 2025 balance suggests a $9.84 million cap for illustration only; the actual certified denominator is needed. The August report has a $219,304 opening-balance difference from preliminary year-end and a $1 published cash/commitment arithmetic difference; reconciliation is necessary, not an inference of wrongdoing.

Employment and institutional accountability

The second-quarter employment report lists 18 agreement rows, including three default or delinquent statuses with $996,228 specified cumulative amounts due. Contract-specific terms matter: a headcount below the reported requirement does not automatically establish noncompliance. The regional payroll count was 62,800 in September, down 0.9% year over year. Neither the labor statistics nor the comments on Advance Pueblo's promotional material establish causal responsibility. The proposed RFP 26-054, reported issued November 18, should be checked directly for official scope and deadlines.

What the public should be able to track

Track prospect origin, qualified leads, site suitability, proposals, approvals, capital disbursement, construction, opening, actual wages, sustained jobs, repayments, tax receipts and long-term maintenance. Report business retention separately from attraction; distinguish transfers from true net new jobs. Protect private applicant and employee information. Use project-level audit trails and confidential contract review where legally appropriate.

Expanded investigation framework

Findings, policy options and open records are differentiated. All recommended measures are proposals, not adopted city requirements.

1–5 · Scope, evidence and institutional history

City fund decisions, County activities and metropolitan employment use different geographies. Stable archive records establish dated financial baselines, while promotional material establishes only the agency's description of its activities. Community concerns about recruitment and preservation are leads, not representative survey evidence. Obtain dated project outcomes and the signed institutional-transition documents before comparing Advance Pueblo and PEDCO performance.

6–10 · Tax, law and accounts

Official 2025 canvass: Question 2B passed 14,848 to 11,257, or 56.88%. The 2026 adopted budget includes $11,722,620 in general taxes, $10,669,749 in projects to be determined and $3,403,970 in transfers out. Preliminary December 2025 cash was $84,272,104, with $18,661,749 committed and $65,610,355 uncommitted. August 2026 reports $84,052,800 opening balance, $10,813,272 receipts and $1,566,906 outflows. Recent-report date conflicts remain unresolved; do not infer wrongdoing.

11–15 · Cap, project commitments, repayment and compliance

Illustrative 2026 cap $9,841,553.25 from preliminary year-end balance, not a certification. St. Charles improvements ($7,983,641), airport terminal ($4,120,333), shell building lot 12 ($2,334,920) and FomCore rent abatement ($1,521,662) represent about 75.5% of listed future commitments. Distinguish commitments from expenditures. Report receipts under shortfalls separately from current amounts due. Some employment rows marked compliant have numerically fewer employees than the stated requirement; only the underlying contracts explain the status.

16–20 · Labor markets, attribution and recruitment

September preliminary resident labor force 71,000; employed 66,700; unemployed 4,300. Colorado's comparable unemployment rate was 4.1%, Colorado Springs 4.5%, Grand Junction 4.4%, Greeley 4.7%. These differ from city-level metrics. External incentive research estimates that a minority of subsidized location decisions are actually induced by incentives; a Pueblo-specific counterfactual is needed. Track outbound and inbound leads, qualified proposals, decisions and lost projects with a consistent cohort denominator.

21–25 · Retention, pipelines, wages, housing and utilities

Record existing-business visits, resolved barriers, survival and net expansions separately from new recruitment. PEDCO's self-reported due-diligence pipeline is a lead list, not verified hiring. Test wage distribution, hours, benefits and resident access. Site selection must account for housing affordability, commuting, water and wastewater capacity, electricity rates, reliability, drainage and required capital improvements.

26–30 · Local enterprise, culture, AI and infrastructure

Compare local procurement, retail leakage and entrepreneurship programs with incoming large-employer subsidies. Cultural assets can have visitor, educational, heritage and placemaking value; use measured impacts and replacement-cost analysis rather than assume a sale or preservation yields larger benefits. Assess advanced manufacturing and AI opportunities against electricity, water, workforce and supply-chain constraints. Treat Pueblo West development as a separate jurisdiction. For roads, airport and TIF projects, verify public ownership, reimbursement, operating costs and maintenance responsibility.

31–35 · Procurement and fiscal evaluation

RFP 26-054 is reported by secondary listings as issued November 18, with questions due December 8 and proposals December 23; verify official solicitation. Proposed evaluation criteria: demonstrated recruitment and retention results 25%, Pueblo-specific strategy 25%, workforce and employer coordination 15%, transparent metrics and reconciliation 20%, cost and contract safeguards 15%. Net fiscal present value equals discounted incremental receipts plus recoveries minus incentives, operating costs and infrastructure maintenance. Test low, base and high scenarios; no local net return has been established. Portfolio options should be compared using fiscal, employment, equity and environmental measures, with eligibility and affordability gates before scoring.

36–40 · Governance, public products, record requests and updates

Recommendation: publish quarterly commitments, payments, sustained jobs, wages, repayments and methodological notes; use independent review for high-value decisions. Provide an accessible project register and correction log. Obtain certified January 1 balances, year-end adjustments, statutory spending classifications, agreement-specific obligations, payout and repayment ledgers, and procurement records. Recheck December procurement milestones and January fiscal balances. Preserve all versions; update the public article without overwriting predecessor evidence. This investigation is open, not a completed audit.
