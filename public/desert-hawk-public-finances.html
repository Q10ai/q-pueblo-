Desert Hawk Golf Course: what public records reveal about costs, debt and regional obligations

County 2024 audited statements show $238,560 operating income but a $1,667,877 negative net position. Pueblo West’s 2025 audit lists $7,613,306 in long-term notes plus a $457,000 land option. Public records are spread across two institutions; no freely transferable balance is established. [S1,S2]

Independent public-source review; not a certified audit or final budget. Screenshot observations, public source findings and Q proposals are distinct.

Findings and processing · 40 research perspectives · Unresolved questions · Public sources · Structured packet · Ask Q

1. Public-source finding and scope

A supplied comment asks why Desert Hawk financial details have not been released. Annual financial details are available, although split across agencies. The county’s latest audited golf statements reviewed here are for 2024; Pueblo West’s 2025 audit supplies a newer creditor-side view. A missing 2025 county report does not erase the published records. [S1,S2,S8]

2. Acquisition and purpose

The county acquired the course in 2000, citing recreation and preservation of area property values. Resolution 01-134, May 8, 2001, established a cooperative management board. The original 2005 acquisition-refinancing COPs were fully retired in 2024. Other obligations survive. [S1,S5]

3. Management and responsibility

The official board registry lists seven seats, shared county/district appointments and the three county commissioners among members. A county enterprise fund reports the golf activity. Board membership is not evidence of personal wrongdoing. [S1,S5,S6]

4. Operating results

2024 customer/service revenue $1,531,007 less operating expenses $1,292,447 equals operating income $238,560 (15.58% margin). Income before transfers is $245,184; $45,322 transfers in raise net-position change to $290,506. Positive operating income does not establish financial independence or cash available to transfer. [S1 p25]

5. Operating expense ledger

Contract fees $535,567; utilities and communications $323,820; supplies $193,504; depreciation $88,897; personnel $49,709; repairs/maintenance $42,995; insurance $30,038; professional services $27,522; equipment rent $395. These total $1,292,447; utilities includes more than water and contracts do not identify individual payees. [S1 p25]

6. Assets, liabilities and net position

Assets $6,740,132 include cash $277,275, investments $898,089, net capital assets $5,076,762 and development land $457,000. Liabilities $8,396,323 plus deferred inflows $11,686 result in net position −$1,667,877; unrestricted net position −$6,699,928. Book values are not appraisals or sellable cash. [S1 p24]

7. Cash flow and retired financing

Operating cash flow $340,307; transfer receipts $45,322; capital/financing cash outflow $730,750 (including $580,000 COP principal); investing net outflow $94,890. The net decline is $440,011, from $717,286 to $277,275 cash. The county narrative says General Fund transfers were not needed for COP repayment in 2024, yet the enterprise statements show a transfer in; origin and purpose require reconciliation, not an assumption of operating subsidy. [S1 pp9,26]

8. County obligation breakdown

At end 2024 county reports $6,334,553 non-interest-bearing loan, $1,279,813 water advance, $457,000 purchase option; total $8,071,366. Other current obligations include $212,715 due to other funds; do not add a second copy of the district receivable to regional debt. [S1 pp24,66]

9. Newer district-side evidence

2025 Pueblo West audit lists $6,078,478 historical operating/debt-service note, $255,000 acquisition/other note and $1,279,828 water note; total notes $7,613,306, plus $457,000 land option = $8,070,306. It reports notes not scheduled to be collected the subsequent year. County 2024 versus district 2025 totals differ by $1,060; dates and classifications differ, and no repayment can be inferred solely from that difference. [S2 p26]

10. Water cost and counterevidence

The IGA treats water not covered by annual revenue before 2011 as noninterest-bearing loans and provides untreated water at no more than actual district cost. District’s May 2026 statement says the golf course has paid under agreement terms since 2011. Hence $8.07M is not an ordinary overdue water bill. Current consumption, rate, metering and repair costs require independent comparison. [S1,S2,S3]

11. Repayment and event horizon

Repayments are limited to excess revenues over cash disbursements and $500,000 per year for all applicable loans. For the 2025 district notes alone (excluding land option), illustrative constant annual payments $100k, $250k, $500k imply 77, 31, 16 payment years, assuming no further advances or changes and all payment directed to those notes. These are bounds/scenarios, not an enforceable amortization schedule. [S1,S2]

12. 2027 budget and decision

County slides project 2026 ending budgetary balance $1.29M, 2027 revenue $1.82M, expense $1.55M and ending $1.56M, all millions. The 2026 inputs are unaudited. An August 19 secondary meeting summary reports equipment reductions and fee decisions; verify original minutes before relying on contract/fee changes. [S4,S7]

13. Water infrastructure and asset choices

Investigate irrigation replacement, lifecycle cost, measured losses and water affordability. Secondary meeting summary reports infrastructure discussion, but no leak estimates or funding claims are treated as verified. Compare retain/reinvest, competitively contract, restructure IGA, lease, sell or change use only after appraisals, legal review, access and debt/land-option protections. [S7]

14. Public safety crossover

Do not count the $1.56M projected golf budgetary balance, the $7.61M notes or the sale book value as freely available sheriff funds. Assess legal authority, obligations, deferred maintenance, debt repayment priorities, revenue loss and recurring net effect separately. Compare golf recreation and affordability, water affordability, emergency resources and child/family effects without assuming service tradeoffs are zero.

15. Q processing, LL, EE and T2Y

Retain fiscal date, entity, fund, accounting basis, item, counterparty, value and source page; match district assets to county liabilities only at common dates. Record public statements as attributed, do not replicate commentary identities. Reuse audited rows across budget, water, debt, regional twin and public asset packets; review contradictions rather than overwriting them.

16. RX, implementation and future

Build an asset/IGA packet with ownership, financing, repayment, transfers, water volumes and lifecycle maintenance; publish costed alternatives and annual totals. Trigger review at 2027 budget adoption, next County ACFR and any IGA amendment, then year 3 and 5 repayment/capital comparisons. Human decision authority remains with participating governments. No automated financial action.

Interactive repayment scenario

Assumes $7,613,306 of district notes, excludes the $457,000 land option and assumes no new advances, unchanged terms, adequate available cash and the entire selected payment applied to notes. Not an approved repayment schedule.
