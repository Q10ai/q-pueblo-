40 substantive perspectives

  1. What this is about

    A pending regulated electric rate case for Pueblo, Cañon City, Rocky Ford and other customers in Black Hills Colorado Electric territory.

    Source 1 · Source 2

  2. Main finding

    The proposed $26,663,479 annual revenue change is not a bill already approved. The 600-kWh example rises $18.30/month without the proposed temporary credit.

    Source 1 · Source 2

  3. History

    The 2024 filing was ultimately reduced; March 2025 reporting describes $17M approved and roughly $7/month residential impact. The new application is separate.

    Source 14

  4. Current status

    June 12 filing; Council opposition October 26; public comments in December and January. Applicant anticipates March 2027, regulator targets spring 2027; actual effective date depends on order.

    Source 1 · Source 3 · Source 4 · Source 6

  5. Dates

    December 1 Cañon City; December 2 Pueblo; January 19, 2027 virtual. Early January is encouraged for written comments; confirm latest schedule.

    Source 5 · Source 6 · Source 7

  6. Affected people

    Over 102,000 regional customer accounts per company notice, not 102,000 people or Pueblo-only households.

    Source 2

  7. Children

    Higher energy costs could reduce disposable income for food, heating and education. No child-level outcomes have been measured.

    Analytical question or evidence gap; no factual finding asserted.

  8. Families and workers

    The example adds $130.80/year with credit or $219.60/year without it. Fixed-income burden depends on actual bills and income.

    Source 2

  9. Neighborhoods

    Different rate territories and housing efficiencies matter. Compare usage-matched bills, not a Pueblo address label or one anecdote.

    Source 2

  10. Fairness

    The proposed fixed charge rises from $8.77 to $12.00; assess low-usage and energy-burden impacts, including renters who cannot modify their homes.

    Source 2

  11. Responsibility

    Black Hills files and operates the system; the Colorado PUC regulates tariffs; Pueblo City Council can oppose and participate but does not set the PUC rate.

    Source 1 · Source 4

  12. Decision makers

    PUC determines the outcome; public comments inform the process. Trial staff and utility consumer advocate participate; docket index also lists city and county intervention motions.

    Source 1 · Source 9

  13. Rules

    The proceeding reviews proposed tariffs and prudent recoverable costs. Obtain current procedural schedule, orders and testimony before assigning approval status.

    Source 1 · Source 2 · Source 7

  14. Commitments

    Applicant reports approximately $184M invested since the last filing. The claim is not equivalent to a verified rate-base addition or an allowed return.

    Source 2 · Source 3

  15. Accountability

    Request utility workpapers, approved revenue requirement, rate-base roll-forward, cost allocation, audit sampling, and service outcomes. Regulator's public docket is the first route; private company records are not automatically public records.

    Source 7 · Source 9

  16. Revenue

    Proposed incremental annual revenue is a system-wide request, not profits, Pueblo-only receipts, or a tax.

    Source 1 · Source 2

  17. Household costs

    600-kWh filed baseline $124.18; temporary-credit example $135.08; no-credit example $142.48. Small commercial examples $277.47, $285.66, $303.99.

    Source 2

  18. Approved amounts

    No final order for the new increase was established as of November 18. Do not substitute the company's requested rate or 2025 decision for the new outcome.

    Source 1 · Source 14

  19. Actual payments

    No ledger of realized 2026–27 collections or individual work orders was reconciled. Request tariff-to-bill sample, actual capital invoices and recovery by account.

    Analytical question or evidence gap; no factual finding asserted.

  20. Costs and benefits

    Utility cites reliability, resilience and operating costs. Measure whether outage minutes, frequency, capacity and safety improve relative to demonstrated spending.

    Source 3

  21. How it works

    Base charges, usage rates and riders interact. Proposal replaces two residential base-energy tiers (12.159 and 15.807 cents/kWh) with 16 cents/kWh, plus other charges and adjustments.

    Source 2

  22. Capacity

    Company identifies Erie Avenue, Pueblo Industrial Park and airport generation infrastructure investments; actual spare distribution capacity is not established.

    Source 3

  23. Quality

    Request annual SAIDI/SAIFI, outage causes, restoration performance and service-quality compliance, with comparable exclusions and years.

    Analytical question or evidence gap; no factual finding asserted.

  24. Maintenance

    Residents request location-specific vegetation trimming records. Request aggregated corridors, completed miles, dates, contractors, labor, actual versus budget, allocation and tariff recovery; redact security-sensitive detail.

    Analytical question or evidence gap; no factual finding asserted.

  25. Obstacles

    Court or regulatory disputes, capital cost, fuel prices, procurement and rate design may affect timelines. Do not infer future delay without procedural evidence.

    Source 7

  26. Connected issues

    Municipal bills, schools, museums, nonprofits, hospitals, housing, public safety and small businesses could face operating-cost pressure. No citywide cost has been measured.

    Source 4

  27. Energy needs

    Separate resource plan case 26A-0333E and economic development case 25A-0500E matter for future power demand and cost allocation. Avoid treating their dollars as additive without a model.

    Source 4 · Source 17 · Source 22

  28. Environment

    Reliability, wildfire mitigation, fuel and renewable resources may carry distinct benefits and costs. Request quantified outcomes and alternatives, not a standalone claim that all spending is necessary.

    Source 3

  29. Risks

    Credit expires; higher base persists if approved as filed. Rate shock can interact with rent, gas, water and insurance increases; no cascading insolvency forecast is established.

    Source 1 · Source 4

  30. Long term

    Compare five- and twenty-year cumulative costs, affordability, debt, technology, reliability, and future grid investment under each provider scenario.

    Source 10 · Source 11

  31. Alternatives

    Regulated incumbent with rate scrutiny, negotiated concessions, energy assistance, weatherization, community solar, cooperative or public-power feasibility, and regional bulk-power options. No immediate supplier switch is established.

    Source 10 · Source 11 · Source 19

  32. Tradeoffs

    City-only utility draft puts acquisition-related total at $383–503M in 2030 dollars and finds city-only municipalization infeasible under assumptions. Black Hills-funded study estimates financing above $1B under its own framework; reconcile scopes rather than merge figures.

    Source 10 · Source 11

  33. Lessons

    Benchmark Xcel, San Isabel Electric and Colorado Springs Utilities using current tariffs, identical usage, all riders, reliability and capitalization. No tariff-normalized ranking was verified here.

    Analytical question or evidence gap; no factual finding asserted.

  34. Improvements

    Publish a plain-language revenue bridge, maintenance accountability matrix, matched-tariff comparator, energy-burden dashboard and assistance navigator; assign review owners before implementation.

    Analytical question or evidence gap; no factual finding asserted.

  35. Public action

    Submit case 26AL-0232E comments and factual examples; ask for written answers. No need to disclose private bill account number in public filings.

    Source 6 · Source 8

  36. Evidence strength

    Regulator confirms proposal and refund mechanics; utility notice supplies bill scenarios. Hearing specifics partly corroborated via local reporting/mayor. Official direct docket review remains incomplete.

    Source 1 · Source 2 · Source 5 · Source 6 · Source 7

  37. Conflicting claims

    Rate approval is not inevitable: prior case was reduced. State statutes limit PUC mandates to remove gas appliances from existing buildings in relevant programs; check local/new-building rules separately. No proof of excess profit or maintenance fraud supplied.

    Source 14 · Source 15

  38. Unknowns

    Current staff and intervenor positions, requested and allowed return on equity, actual vegetation cost and location data, rate-class allocation, city facility costs, and final order.

    Source 7 · Source 9

  39. Change triggers

    New testimony, settlement, commission order, revised tariff, hearing changes, or a validated municipalization update trigger amendments; historical baseline stays intact.

    Source 7 · Source 16

  40. Qified insight

    The temporary credit, permanent base-rate design, system costs and household outcomes must be tracked separately. Pair evidence-led intervention with source-backed local relief and long-term scenario comparisons.
