History & heritage

How a city grows and changes

Pueblo grew from separate municipalities, which consolidated in 1886 and 1894. Historical maps and ordinances help explain why communities retain distinct identities. To tell the history accurately, compare dated documents with photographs and first-person accounts; distinguish observed facts from later interpretations.

Nature, biology & earth science

Our region is a connected ecosystem

Grasslands, river habitats and woodlands support different plants and animals. A watershed is the land draining to a common outlet. Rain, soil, vegetation and land use affect runoff; fire can change how quickly water reaches streams. A historical flood does not determine current parcel-level risk.

Schools & education systems

Who is responsible for a child's education when a school changes?

School districts, charter authorizers, education cooperatives, state agencies and contractors have different responsibilities. When an authorization ends, families need clear information about enrollment, student supports, records and continuity. Public decisions require transparent procedures, regardless of the participants' beliefs. Read the two linked investigations to see how these responsibilities played out in Pueblo.

Government, law & citizenship

Who can make which decisions?

City, county, state and federal agencies have different responsibilities. An agenda announces proposed business; a vote records a decision; an adopted ordinance may establish authority. A contract and payment are separate later steps. Identify jurisdiction and the actual controlling record before judging a decision.

Mathematics, graphs & data

Numbers tell different stories depending on the denominator

A percentage compares a part with a whole. If 25 dollars of 100 authorized dollars are documented as paid, the ratio is 25 percent. This does not establish the remaining cash. A graph should label units, period, denominator and financial stage rather than mixing totals with their components.

Money, economics & everyday finances

Follow one dollar through its life

A household plans a budget, agrees to expenses, pays bills and checks what it received. Public finance has additional legal controls. An appropriation is spending authority; an obligation is a commitment; actual expenditure needs a matching record. Compare resource use and useful results instead of treating a promise as a benefit.

Engineering, energy & infrastructure

Why a bridge is more than concrete

Infrastructure begins with a problem and alternatives, then proceeds through design, approvals, construction and maintenance. Electrical demand is power in MW; energy accumulates over time in MWh. Costs depend on operating hours, utilization and rates as well as construction. A proposal is not a working facility.

AI, computing & digital literacy

AI can assist without becoming the decision-maker

Language models generate responses from patterns in data and context. They may offer useful explanations but also invent details or confuse dates. A reliable workflow begins with an exact question, gathers evidence, checks contradictions and leaves consequential decisions with accountable people. Use only permitted data.

Health, safety & human services

Learn to distinguish information from a diagnosis

Public health looks at populations, prevention, environmental conditions and access to services. A dated public notice is different from a current safety determination or a clinical assessment of one person. Individual health decisions need qualified care; emergencies need appropriate emergency services.

Art, culture, language & media

How people preserve and tell stories

A photograph, oral history, mural, song or museum exhibit can convey experience and cultural meaning. Ask who created it, for whom, and what context is missing. Respect copyright and cultural permissions; a link is not permission to reproduce. Interpretations may differ without changing a verifiable date.

Connect useful skills to real work

A career combines knowledge, practice and responsibilities. A job title is not enough to understand daily work, training or safety. Compare public job descriptions and education pathways, then check local requirements and conditions. National wage figures are not necessarily Pueblo wages.

Preparedness, environment & problem-solving

How communities handle disruptions

A resilient system has more than one safe way to deliver essential services. During a disruption, safety and continuity come before convenience. Identify dependencies, backup options, responsible people and the evidence needed to know a service is restored. Use official current guidance for actual emergencies.
