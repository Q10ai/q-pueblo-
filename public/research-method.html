How do we know a public claim is true?

A search result is a clue. A public record is evidence. A source-checked answer explains the evidence, the dates, the disagreement, and what's still unknown. AI can help organize information, but people decide what to publish.

Five checks before we publish an answer

  1. Find the original. Who published the record, and can you open the actual document?
  2. Check the date and jurisdiction. Does it describe Pueblo, the county, a district, or something else?
  3. Check the exact claim. Is the number a request, an appropriation, a contract, a payment, or a measured result?
  4. Look for differences. Is there an updated official record, a correction, or evidence that contradicts it?
  5. Document the answer. Show what is known, what remains unknown, source links, the review date, and ways to correct it.

Example: An approved budget is not proof that the money was spent. An election result is not proof that a new law, tax collection or public project has already taken effect.

What Q Pueblo can and cannot do

Available: read dated public research pages and source links, use browser-based tools, and send questions or corrections by an email you control.

Not established: live independent AI search agents, automated fact certification, continuous real-time source collection or automatic publication.

A report may say draft, stale, disputed or insufficient evidence. That is useful information, not a system error.
