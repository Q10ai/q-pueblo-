Fiber access: ownership, permits and service

Case summary · captured 2026-11-10 · original evidence dates apply

Fiber internet is being developed in Pueblo. Intrepid's local project page identifies T-Fiber as an ordering partner and distinguishes planned construction, engineering and permitting, construction, and service availability. Those stages matter: a fiber line in the neighborhood does not mean an individual address can order service.

Construction, right-of-way permits, network ownership, retail service and street restoration involve different responsibilities. The local provider page supplies a construction contact, 1-844-380-8090. Actual address availability, contract terms and restoration acceptance remain unverified in this study.

Library AI: contracts, privacy, costs and useful outcomes

Case summary · captured 2026-11-10 · original evidence dates apply

Pueblo City-County Library District announced a partnership with BoodleBox in its September 2026 program guide, with library staff training first and a September 22 public launch program. This is a real local example of AI literacy moving into a public institution. It does not establish that every patron receives an account or that a particular paid subscription is included.

BoodleBox's free Basic tier is a separate consumer option with usage limits. The library's partnership raises practical questions about training, accessibility, student and patron privacy, contract cost, retention and exit. The institution's announcement is evidence of the partnership, not independent proof of the vendor's reported resource savings or an executed contract's terms.

The useful measure is whether staff and patrons can learn more safely and achieve better results with less time, energy and expense. Contract invoices, actual energy use and measured learning outcomes remain unverified.

Library finance: audited records, services and facility use

Case summary · captured 2026-11-10 · original evidence dates apply

The library's November 10 preliminary 2027 budget projects General Fund revenue of $17.44 million, spending of $16.00 million, and a $2.15 million transfer to its capital project fund. Its projected General Fund balance declines from an estimated $5.64 million at the end of 2026 to $4.93 million at the end of 2027—a decrease of about $0.71 million using the rounded published balances. The capital fund balance moves in the opposite direction, so these figures must not be added as independent spending.

The district's two funds together project approximately $17.72 million of revenue, $17.48 million of expenditures and $10.39 million of ending fund balance. The preliminary budget lists $678,080 for General Fund IT and $481,000 in capital IT. Those broad categories do not reveal the cost of BoodleBox or other individual contracts.

The 2027 estimated levy of 6.4726 mills is not certification. The November 13 county election snapshot lists 6A as 39,173 yes and 26,654 no, unofficially; later controlling records determine the outcome and effective levy. Budget projections, ballot results and actual service delivery are distinct.

Passenger rail: station plans, owners and service commitments

Case summary · captured 2026-11-10 · original evidence dates apply

Passenger rail planning connects a proposed service with local station access, host railroads, district governance and funding. The Front Range Passenger Rail District describes its delivery plan and a November 3 ballot question. It specifically directs readers to aggregated results across all twelve participating counties.

Pueblo County's November 13 unofficial result for 7A was 20,599 yes and 18,114 no. That is the Pueblo County portion only, not the districtwide result. Even a certified ballot result would not by itself establish a train timetable, executed host agreement or station opening date. For travelers, those operating details are the outcomes that matter.

Railroad-tie fire: evidence, material and jurisdiction questions

Case summary · captured 2026-11-10 · original evidence dates apply

The archived case concerns a reported September 5 railroad-tie fire in Pueblo's Midtown area. The earlier research names municipal fire and code functions along with environmental agencies. The linked September news accounts describe the incident; federal reference material explains creosote generally, not the specific materials or exposure at this site.

An incident, the actual composition of burning material, possible exposure, cleanup and site responsibility are different questions. The archived evidence does not establish current conditions, measured exposure, a legal violation or a responsible party. For immediate hazards, follow current official emergency and public-health notices.

School authorization and governance: roles and public records

Case summary · captured 2026-11-10 · original evidence dates apply

School governance determines who authorizes a school, administers its operations, receives funds and is accountable for student services. An online school authorization can differ from a local district's responsibilities. Earlier Pueblo research identifies Riverstone Academy, Education reEnvisioned BOCES, District 70 and state education records as distinct leads.

The archived sources include a state announcement about transfer of online-school authorization to District 49. That announcement is not proof of a particular Pueblo school's current authorization or a finding of wrongdoing. Families need the applicable enrollment and authorizer records for the relevant school year. This case needs a fresh school-specific source review.

Connecting Communities: trail funding and delivery

Case summary · captured 2026-11-10 · original evidence dates apply

Connecting Communities trail planning concerns how people might travel between neighborhoods and public destinations. The archived research identifies regional planning, City and County participation, a transportation funding record and an April 2026 design solicitation.

Funding announcements, design procurement and completed accessible trails are different stages. A trail can improve access to jobs, recreation and services, but actual benefits depend on the route, access points, safety, maintenance and completion. This archive does not establish final construction cost, accepted trail mileage or opening dates.
