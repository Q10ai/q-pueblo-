How Q checks and interprets information
  1. Understand the person: their real question, needs, rights, scope and possible consequences.
  2. Find evidence: original records, dates, institutions, contradictions and gaps.
  3. QIFY: crosswalk law, finance, energy, environment, social effects, technology and risks without inventing facts.
  4. Test alternatives: establish baselines, simulations and failure modes, all explicitly labeled.
  5. Deliver useful work: a research report, evidence table, decision aid, contact map, learning guide or Q-RX option—not another unsupported promise.
  6. Human review and exit: people remain in control, can seek corrections and use appropriately portable results.

How AI affects our region

AI in education

Use AI to explain the same Pueblo history concept at different reading levels, then compare the explanations to the original archive. A teacher retains responsibility for assessment; a model must not decide a student's grade or placement.

Try: Explain the 1921 flood in three paragraphs for a middle-school class, with one primary-source link and a question that tests understanding.

AI in governance

AI can summarize agendas and compare budget versions. It must distinguish a proposal, vote, executed contract and payment; public officials remain responsible for decisions. Never put private case files into consumer AI tools.

Try: Compare the City and County budgets using the same fiscal stages. Explain which figures can be compared and which cannot.

AI in healthcare

AI may help explain public health notices, medical vocabulary and questions for a clinician. It cannot diagnose an individual from a generic prompt or substitute for current official emergency guidance. Patient data and clinical decisions require approved systems and qualified oversight.

Try: Explain why a repaired wastewater system and a current water-safety determination are different, without making a new safety claim.

AI in businesses and jobs

A local business can test AI for drafting public product descriptions, process checklists and inventory analysis using non-sensitive sample data. Compare minutes saved, corrections, energy and service quality against the old process before expanding.

Try: Design a low-cost one-week pilot for a small Pueblo business that measures staff time, error rate and review cost. Do not promise job or financial returns.

AI infrastructure, energy and water

Data centers turn electrical power into computing and heat. Cooling, grid capacity, water, network access, land-use authority and public financing can all matter. A site proposal is not a utility commitment or a construction approval. Compare facility power, utilization, energy price and measured water consumption before calculating impacts.

Try: Compare a hypothetical 10 MW and 50 MW facility at 80% utilization; show annual electricity use and explain what remains unknown about local grid, cooling and water capacity.

AI in everyday life

Use AI for language explanations, planning and learning, not as an authority on your eligibility for assistance or a replacement for personal judgment. A public resource link and a phone number are usually more useful than a confident guess about local availability.

Try: Find the official Pueblo food assistance programs, distinguish eligibility and enrollment, and list the phone or application routes without asking for sensitive information.
