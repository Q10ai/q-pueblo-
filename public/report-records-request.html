Getting the records that could resolve a disputed public-spending claim

Evidence checked 2026-11-09 · Check the listed review date.

Findings and more detail · Sources

Short answer

A focused request for existing authorizations, agreements and payment records is more useful than asking a custodian to prove a broad allegation. Colorado’s legislative legal summary explains CORA, custodians, exceptions and potential research/retrieval charges; the current custodian policy and applicable law still control.

The full story

Why a record matters

Public spending disputes are often easier to resolve when the specific decision, contract or payment is identified. Colorado's legislative summary describes access to existing public records, subject to the applicable law and exceptions.

How a request works

The responsible custodian handles existing records. A request for a specific dated agreement or transaction is different from asking the office to produce a new investigation. Research and retrieval fees may apply.

Practical help

Ask for an estimate before authorizing chargeable work. The legal summary below explains the general process. This report has not submitted a request or determined the legal outcome of a particular case.

What Q finds

The most useful first step is to define the proposition that needs testing and request the existing records capable of testing it. A document return is evidence to analyze, not an automatic verdict.

What the available evidence cannot establish

  • A custodian may lawfully withhold or redact material, may not hold the record, or may use another process. Those possibilities must be recorded before inferring concealment.
