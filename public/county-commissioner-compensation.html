Who sets commissioner pay — and how does it compare with county workers?

The 2024 publication lists $131,700, $125,916 and $131,700. The state schedule assigns Pueblo Category I-A, with $158,669 annually for new terms starting in 2026–27, not every incumbent’s verified payroll. [S1,S2]

Independent educational public-source review, not a certified audit or government finding. Evidence status and proposals are distinguished below; missing information does not prevent publication of available findings.

Findings and processing · Unresolved questions · Original sources · Download structured packets

1. What the original payroll publication says

The three listed commissioner rows total $389,316. The same page lists county manager $221,460, county attorney $180,000 and several different administrator rates. These are published salary entries, not independently reconciled payroll or benefits. [S1]

2. Who establishes the rate

Colorado's elected county official salary framework under C.R.S. 30-2-102 applies category and term-specific schedules. The September 2026 state memo includes Pueblo in I-A and sets $158,669 for commissioners whose terms begin in 2026 or 2027. Generally compensation cannot change during an elected term, subject to applicable legal provisions. [S2]

3. Recalculate the social-media claim

$158,669 minus $131,700 equals $26,969 (20.48%); compared with $125,916 the difference is $32,753 (26.01%). These compare different published rates/periods; neither proves that all three current commissioners received the same raise or personally voted for it. [S1,S2]

4. Comparable state rates

Table 1 also lists Category I-A sheriff $201,926 and treasurer, assessor and clerk $158,669 for the relevant new terms. Avoid treating all officials as already being paid the new rate. [S2]

5. Household comparison

$158,669 is 3.17 times a hypothetical $50,000 annual wage. Three such commissioner salaries would total $476,007 before benefits, a scenario not current payroll. Compared with the 2024 listed total, the difference would be $86,691, not a measure of lawful savings. [S1,S2]

6. Compensation versus funding

The preliminary budget identifies department reduction targets, not individual wage reductions. The elected office may meet its target in different spending categories. Compare authorized compensation, funded position, actual salary, overtime, employer benefit and vacancy separately. [S5]

7. Counterpoint

Local affordability is a valid public-policy question even when the statutory rate is set by the state. The compensation mechanism, board's local budget decisions and actual employee treatment are different questions.

8. Community signal

Visible responses include concerns about executive compensation, deputy safety, ability to afford taxes and a self-reported difficulty obtaining county employment. The hiring account is unverified; no conclusion about discrimination, intent or personal enrichment follows.

9. Hiring and benefits

Analyze public job postings, pay bands, eligibility, vacancies, selection rules and aggregate retention before judging hiring fairness. Compare pension participation using plan documents and aggregate contributions; never infer individual enrollment or disclose protected records.

10. Child and economic impacts

Model changes in household income, childcare affordability and spending at local businesses if workforce reductions occur; distinguish positions from people and authorized from actual reductions.

11. Q processing and build

Use year, title, appointment/term start, salary basis, benefit basis, fund, source and confidence. Make term schedules a separate dataset joined to office positions only when effective-date evidence is known. Public view must not identify private employees beyond authorized disclosures.

12. EE / T2Y / LL

Calculate changes once from preserved source rows; compare similarly scoped compensation across years and institutions. Avoid repeating the viral claim as verified. Retain the original concern as a public-signal record.

13. RX and horizons

Verify 2025/2026 public disclosures, the applicable effective rate for each office and February 2027 reporting. For 2029 and 2031 assess affordability against comparable workloads and local wage distributions, not arbitrary executive-pay ceilings.
