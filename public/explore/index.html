01 · Q Wiki

Read a claim. Inspect a source. Understand the version.

A wiki entry is an explanation and a navigational layer, never its own primary evidence. It should link to original documents, counterevidence, review receipts, and earlier versions.

Evidence grammar

  • Source: an identifiable original or attributed record.
  • Claim: a proposition with a date and evidence state.
  • Perspective: an attributed interpretation, experience or concern.
  • Inference: reasoning whose assumptions can be challenged.
  • Scenario: an explicitly hypothetical alternative, not a forecast.
  • Outcome: something actually observed and independently validated at an identified date.

Why a living wiki?

The latest narrative must not silently erase what people knew before. Every correction should preserve the original claim, the reason for change, review authority, the superseded version and a new review/TTL trigger.

A quote or screenshot is not automatically current or verified. Absence of evidence must remain UNKNOWN rather than FALSE.

03 · Regional City Shadow

A time-indexed picture, not a claim of live omniscience.

The eventual regional shadow can connect approved aggregates about transportation, public facilities, budgets and infrastructure over time. Household addresses, protected child locations and personal dossiers are excluded.

Past → Present → Future

Past: archived source versions, remembered conditions and dated decisions.

Present: approved observations with freshness indicators and unresolved gaps.

Future: bounded possibilities, assumptions, intervals and triggers for review.

Risk & resource accounting

Source publication, observation, event and approval timestamps stay separate. Financial amounts preserve requested/proposed/appropriated/contracted/paid/outcome stages. Energy, labor, attention and maintenance are measured when possible; otherwise UNKNOWN.

No real-time feed or digital-twin validation is represented on this page.

04 · Multiple perspectives and evolving scenarios

Learn to compare different truths people experience—and one evidentiary record.

Explore four fictional case studies, each with five stakeholder lenses and three decision branches. These lenses are not quotes or positions of real officials, organizations or residents.

01FICTIONAL · INFRASTRUCTURE
Repair a busy industrial road

A fictional community must decide how and when to repair a road used by trucks, workers, and families.

Not a real project finding. These five lenses are fictional analytical viewpoints. The alternatives are unranked; no outcomes or probabilities have been established.

Five points of view

Family / student

Priority: Reliable access to schools and care

Possible benefit: Safer travel routes and fewer disruptions

Possible burden: Construction detours can increase travel time

What evidence would help? Which routes do children and caregivers actually use?

Child lens: Past access barriers, present crossings, future mobility

Worker / commuter

Priority: Safety and predictable shifts

Possible benefit: Fewer vehicle hazards and delays

Possible burden: Temporary access disruptions can affect wages

What evidence would help? When do people travel, and what alternate routes exist?

Child lens: Parents may bear shift and childcare disruptions

Business / freight

Priority: Reliable industrial access

Possible benefit: Lower damage and transit uncertainty

Possible burden: Short-term deliveries may be disrupted

What evidence would help? What are the freight volumes and verified cost exposures?

Child lens: Local jobs, air exposure and future business resilience

Public works / finance

Priority: Legal authority, useful-life cost and maintenance

Possible benefit: Coordinated durable repair

Possible burden: Other essential projects may face tradeoffs

What evidence would help? Who owns the road and which funds are legally eligible?

Child lens: Long-run safety and intergenerational public cost

Environmental / neighborhood

Priority: Air, noise and access equity

Possible benefit: Possible reduction of vehicle idling

Possible burden: Work may generate dust, traffic and noise

What evidence would help? What baseline air/noise readings exist?

Child lens: Disproportionate burdens on children close to the route

Alternative futures — none predetermined

Repair now

Potential benefit: Potentially addresses current hazards

Tradeoff: Construction disruption and upfront cost

Dependency: Valid jurisdiction and appropriated funds

Child future: Safer future routes if design works

How to measure: Injury data and post-repair maintenance cost

Phase repairs and safety controls

Potential benefit: May spread cost and maintain some access

Tradeoff: Hazards may persist during phases

Dependency: Verified hazard ranking and monitored controls

Child future: Children may benefit later, but interim risks remain

How to measure: Phase-specific access and incident trends

Defer and monitor

Potential benefit: Preserves immediate funds

Tradeoff: Maintenance and safety risks may grow

Dependency: Evidence that near-term conditions meet required standards

Child future: Potentially transfers cost and risk to future residents

How to measure: Safety inspections and worsening pavement condition

Child Impact Statement

Past: Review prior road design, historical access and maintenance decisions.

Present: Evaluate current travel safety, access and displacement of risks.

Future: Consider 5, 10, 25 and 50-year safety, budgets and mobility.

Source context for a real investigation: City public works and council archives / Recorded transportation and safety studies. These are research starting points, not evidence for the fictional case.

02FICTIONAL · PUBLIC MONEY
Should a city support a major employer?

A fictional public incentive proposal promises jobs but also requires taxpayer support and new infrastructure.

Not a real project finding. These five lenses are fictional analytical viewpoints. The alternatives are unranked; no outcomes or probabilities have been established.

Five points of view

Worker / job seeker

Priority: Wages, access and training

Possible benefit: Potential jobs and skills

Possible burden: Jobs may differ from forecasts

What evidence would help? What pay, benefits and realized employment are documented?

Child lens: Parents and future students need accessible career paths

Taxpayer / household

Priority: Affordability and accountable spending

Possible benefit: Potential future tax base

Possible burden: Debt, utility and opportunity costs may be shifted

What evidence would help? What is authorized versus actually paid?

Child lens: Public services used by children compete for resources

Existing small business

Priority: Competition and local supply chains

Possible benefit: More regional customers

Possible burden: Unequal incentives could disadvantage incumbents

What evidence would help? Which local suppliers benefit and at what cost?

Child lens: Long-term local ownership and apprenticeship opportunities

Public finance / government

Priority: Legal use, performance and clawbacks

Possible benefit: Conditional investment tied to outcomes

Possible burden: Monitoring or fiscal failure

What evidence would help? What approvals, terms and clawbacks actually exist?

Child lens: Who inherits maintenance obligations and costs?

Neighborhood / environment

Priority: Land, water, housing and traffic

Possible benefit: Potential amenities and infrastructure upgrades

Possible burden: Environmental exposure or housing pressure

What evidence would help? What independent assessments and mitigation plans exist?

Child lens: Long-term water, air and neighborhood conditions

Alternative futures — none predetermined

Approve conditional support

Potential benefit: Could accelerate jobs and construction

Tradeoff: Public cost if milestones fail

Dependency: Independent financial review, measurable jobs and enforceable terms

Child future: Future children could inherit assets or liabilities

How to measure: Observed payroll and audited payments

Negotiate smaller or different package

Potential benefit: Could reduce exposure and improve conditions

Tradeoff: Project might change scope or location

Dependency: Evidence of counteroffers and legal eligibility

Child future: Could balance educational investment and employment

How to measure: Signed revised terms and realized outcomes

Decline support

Potential benefit: Avoids direct incentive burden

Tradeoff: Potential economic opportunity forgone

Dependency: Credible no-action baseline and legal review

Child future: Other public priorities could retain resources

How to measure: Employment baseline and opportunity cost

Child Impact Statement

Past: Identify older commitments and whether earlier benefits materialized.

Present: Separate proposed, authorized, obligated and spent money.

Future: Model job durability, fiscal liabilities, skills and environmental burden.

Source context for a real investigation: City Council finance and agenda records / Audited financial statements and signed agreements. These are research starting points, not evidence for the fictional case.

03FICTIONAL · HISTORY & HEALTH
What should happen at an old industrial site?

A fictional former industrial parcel has incomplete historical records and disputed ideas for future use.

Not a real project finding. These five lenses are fictional analytical viewpoints. The alternatives are unranked; no outcomes or probabilities have been established.

Five points of view

Long-term resident / oral historian

Priority: Recognition of lived history

Possible benefit: Local knowledge can guide research

Possible burden: Memories may lack independently dated corroboration

What evidence would help? What historical records confirm or contextualize oral accounts?

Child lens: Children inherit both place memory and exposure concerns

Public health / clinician

Priority: Safe, defensible evidence

Possible benefit: Appropriate exposure evaluation

Possible burden: False reassurance or unwarranted alarm

What evidence would help? What samples and pathways are scientifically valid?

Child lens: Age-specific exposure pathways and future health

Property owner / developer

Priority: Viable, lawful reuse

Possible benefit: New housing or business opportunities

Possible burden: Remediation and legal costs

What evidence would help? What land-use and cleanup standards actually apply?

Child lens: Future residents need durable protections

Child / family advocate

Priority: Safety, belonging and access

Possible benefit: Could provide safe shared spaces

Possible burden: Unrecognized exposure or exclusion

What evidence would help? Are exposure and accessibility independently reviewed?

Child lens: Place-sensitive, age-specific and multigenerational impacts

Archivist / researcher

Priority: Provenance and uncertainty

Possible benefit: Better historical understanding

Possible burden: Incomplete maps or mistaken identification

What evidence would help? How do we reconcile conflicting parcel names and dates?

Child lens: Preserve honest history without stigma

Alternative futures — none predetermined

Investigate before major reuse

Potential benefit: Improves evidence before irreversible choices

Tradeoff: Time and investigative expense

Dependency: Qualified environmental and rights review

Child future: Could prevent exposure across generations

How to measure: Verified sampling, record access and correction events

Introduce bounded interim protections

Potential benefit: May reduce plausible exposure

Tradeoff: May affect land access and costs

Dependency: Clear legal authority and proportionate safeguards

Child future: Near-term precaution while preserving future options

How to measure: Access and exposure monitoring

Proceed with conditioned reuse

Potential benefit: May create community value

Tradeoff: Unresolved conditions could harm users

Dependency: Demonstrated regulatory, technical and human clearance

Child future: Long-horizon health and remediation obligations

How to measure: Independent compliance and post-use monitoring

Child Impact Statement

Past: Recover prior land use, permits, historic mapping and residents’ accounts.

Present: Assess known and unknown current exposure without inferring diagnosis.

Future: Track stewardship duties, groundwater, housing and child-health horizons.

Source context for a real investigation: Environmental agencies and qualified historic archives / County parcel and land use records. These are research starting points, not evidence for the fictional case.

04FICTIONAL · INFORMATION SAFETY
A viral claim about a local official

A fictional social post makes a serious accusation. Many repost it, but the original evidence is unclear.

Not a real project finding. These five lenses are fictional analytical viewpoints. The alternatives are unranked; no outcomes or probabilities have been established.

Five points of view

Resident / reader

Priority: Truth and fair participation

Possible benefit: Better civic literacy

Possible burden: Amplifying unverified accusations can harm people

What evidence would help? Which documents independently support the claim?

Child lens: Families and children may be harmed by false allegations

Reporter / editor

Priority: Verification and public interest

Possible benefit: Verified investigative reporting

Possible burden: Rushing can misstate context

What evidence would help? What primary records and direct responses have been checked?

Child lens: Long-term trust in local information

Person named in claim

Priority: Fair process and privacy

Possible benefit: Opportunity to correct the record

Possible burden: Misidentification and harassment

What evidence would help? Has the subject been afforded appropriate response?

Child lens: Avoid exposing the person’s children or household

Records custodian

Priority: Authenticity and lawful release

Possible benefit: Authenticated accessible records

Possible burden: Redactions may be misunderstood

What evidence would help? What is the original signed record and its revision history?

Child lens: Future use of records requires privacy safeguards

Teacher / youth educator

Priority: Critical thinking without targeting

Possible benefit: Learn how misinformation spreads

Possible burden: Students may mistake repetition for proof

What evidence would help? Can we teach with fictional examples rather than real names?

Child lens: Protect learner identity and emotional safety

Alternative futures — none predetermined

Reshare immediately

Potential benefit: May spread an important concern if accurate

Tradeoff: Could amplify misinformation and privacy harm

Dependency: Independent verification not yet available

Child future: Children may inherit lasting digital harm

How to measure: Independent original-source count

Verify then contextualize

Potential benefit: Improves accuracy and procedural fairness

Tradeoff: Requires time and careful redaction

Dependency: Primary documents and meaningful response opportunity

Child future: Protects future trust and families

How to measure: Source-lineage and correction audit

Pause amplification and seek records

Potential benefit: Reduces immediate exposure

Tradeoff: May delay legitimate discussion

Dependency: Available, safe public-records process

Child future: Reduces unnecessary reputational harm

How to measure: New original evidence and response records

Child Impact Statement

Past: Recover original posting time, context and older corrections.

Present: Distinguish reposts from independent sources and avoid doxxing.

Future: Observe whether corrections reach the original audience over time.

Source context for a real investigation: Original public records / Professional verification and editorial standards. These are research starting points, not evidence for the fictional case.

Quantum language: These branching futures are conventional scenario analysis, not quantum computation, probability claims, or proof that contradictory factual claims are equally supported.
