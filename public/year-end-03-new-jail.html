Pueblo's new jail: Can the county afford to operate the facility?

Dated research candidate · November 18, 2026

Findings, attributed reports and Q scenarios are distinct. Not an independent completed audit or a current official status. Recheck 2026-11-25; feature through December 31, then reassess for 2027. Source-level freshness controls. Historical reports remain accessible.

Key evidence and current update

The county November progress register distinguishes the new detention center's first operating year from old-facility mothballing. Annual operation, legacy-site upkeep and construction are different cost categories; current staffed intake capacity remains unverified.

Article

The new detention facility is not simply a construction project; it is a continuing commitment to staff, healthcare, maintenance, utilities, transportation and due process. The proposed municipal inmate agreement raises a question about the difference between installed beds and safely staffed beds. No current jail census, unit capacity or confirmed residual capacity has been obtained in this review.

Evidence and history

The county's 2027 budget page contains the preliminary budget and financial outlook. Its November progress update describes fiscal stabilization. KRDO reports a proposed municipal custody agreement. Retrieve capital authorization, financing and final project cost, transition records, occupancy and staffing series; do not conflate capital cost and annual service cost.

Twin model and FFF

Safe intake capacity = minimum of staffed, classification-compatible, medical-supported and legally operational capacity minus occupied beds and reserve allowance. Use validated facility inputs and publish aggregates only. Annual lifecycle expense = personnel + overtime + healthcare + utilities + upkeep + debt service where allocated + equipment reserve; reconcile to fund accounting and avoid duplicate debt counts.

Sensitivity

Illustrative 20 allocated beds × 75% use × 365 = 5,475 bed-days. At hypothetical $60 reimbursement and $100 marginal cost, $328,500 revenue and $547,500 cost leave negative $219,000 before step-change costs. These are examples, not contract values.

Options and Q Rx

Compare capped intake, flexible intake with sheriff suspension, negotiated cost-based rate, deferred intake pending staffing study, and noncustodial alternatives. Require clear admissions, medical responsibility and extraordinary-cost terms, classification protocols and a capacity reserve.

Children and human impact

Protect detainee medical care and due process, victims' safety, continuity of caregiving and workers' health. Consider family communication and referral needs without exposing private records.

EE / T2Y / LEAP

Meter building energy and maintenance against occupancy; use preventive maintenance, avoid unnecessary compute infrastructure. Track staffed beds, overtime, medical costs, length of stay and recidivism definitions quarterly through 2028.

Shadow and event horizons

Even with empty cells, understaffing or clinical needs can constrain intake. Trigger review on validated staffing shortfalls, healthcare disruption, sustained overtime, breached budget or rights incidents. Human operational authority may suspend intake under agreed contract terms.

Records and next actions

Request final cost, debt schedules, current nonidentifying census, minimum staffing methodology, medical contract and IGA. Review after budget amendments and proposed vote. Do not label physical capacity as spare capacity.

Q processing and scenario

20 beds × 75% utilization × 365 = 5,475 bed-days; hypothetical $60 receipt and $100 marginal expense imply $219,000 unrecovered annually, excluding additional staff and unusual medical costs. Use actual contractual amounts when known.

Red-team and release gates

Test classification mismatch, illness surge and overtime escalation. An empty physical cell does not establish a staffed bed.

Tools and builds — actual versus proposed

Detention capacity twin specification: aggregate installed, staffed, classification-compatible and medically supported beds; occupied bed-days, reserve, booked admissions, length of stay and cost stages. Public-safe aggregates only. No live jail system connected.

Existing Q workbench · Records planner. Proposed modules require data, funding, testing and authorization.

What you can do next

Ask for the final construction closeout, actual annual O&M, medical contract, operating capacity definition and city-county agreement.
