Made in Pueblo: what is built, proposed and still to be decided

Pueblo already manufactures products sold beyond the region. Four current stories show the possibilities and the hard questions: industrial payroll, food production, publicly supported infrastructure, water, power and measurable results.

Evidence cut-off: November 20, 2026. A dated research edition, not a live project tracker or an endorsement. Claims of future capital and jobs are not observed outcomes.

The answer in context

Production businesses bring goods to market and can bring revenue from customers outside Pueblo. That supports primary employment and supplier demand; local retail, services, schools, healthcare and government also contribute to the region's wellbeing. The stronger claim that manufacturing alone supports Pueblo's entire economy has not been established.

U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics metropolitan payroll data reported approximately 5,000 Pueblo-area manufacturing payroll jobs in August 2026, with no reported year-over-year change at the published precision. That is an employment baseline, not an accounting of the four proposed projects.

Four projects, four different evidence states

The figures below are sourced projections or descriptions. They must not be summed as firm commitments, actual payroll or approved spending.

Little Leaf Farms — greenhouse proposal

Reported: $150 million proposed first phase, possible approximately $100 million second phase, more than 200 projected primary jobs and average wages approaching $59,000. An employment agreement and lease were under Council consideration for November 23.

Not yet established: final vote, binding jobs and wages by year, public payments, construction start, delivered crops, water demand and operating results. January 2027 groundbreaking was a target, not completion.

Council item tracker · Reporting and document links

Ecoasis AgTech Park — land-use proposal

Reported: a proposed 272-acre campus south of Pueblo, about 160 acres of greenhouses, an associated limited-footprint data center, and approximately 1,000 jobs at full build-out. Developers describe recovering data-center heat for greenhouse use.

Not yet established: county land-use approval, financing, water rights and actual consumption, grid capacity, enforceable job numbers, executed land arrangements and environmental safeguards. A November 16 public information session was reported.

KRDO: proposal and community concerns · HortiDaily project description

Project Falcon — manufacturing prospect

Reported: a potential approximately $800 million multiphase industrial development and about 293 permanent jobs, with wages around $80,000 on average. The City reportedly approved a land option October 26 after earlier negotiations stalled. A new nonbinding MOU was described as upcoming November 23 business.

Not yet established: final MOU adoption, factory construction, completed property transaction, cash incentive approval or disbursement, infrastructure obligations and operational jobs. A land option is not a factory.

Driven Plastics — operating technology and road use

Documented activity: a Pueblo company supplies recycled plastic as an asphalt additive. Public reporting describes Siloam Road testing and inclusion in the Medal of Honor Boulevard roadway, which opened April 10, 2026. This is an implementation example rather than a speculative factory announcement.

Still to measure: independent lifecycle performance, cost per lane-mile, wear under traffic, pavement maintenance savings, environmental tradeoffs and net commercial sales. A demonstration is not itself proof of long-run savings.

Follow the money — without confusing stages

For each project, separate requested → approved → contracted → disbursed → spent → verified outcome. Reported development values represent estimated private investment, not public grant checks. Reported jobs at full build-out are not hiring counts. PEDCO's Half-Cent incentives require a separately evaluated agreement and public approvals; do not assume payment because a project appears on an agenda.

Who is involved?

Developers and investors propose and finance projects. Pueblo City Council acts on City ordinances, agreements and relevant municipal incentives. County land-use decisions belong to County authorities when land is outside City jurisdiction. PEDCO promotes primary-job development; PuebloPlex, chambers, PURA, schools and colleges may have distinct supporting roles. A partnership mention does not establish a legal approval or a financial commitment.
