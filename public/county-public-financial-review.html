What Pueblo County’s published numbers already reveal about the budget crisis

The 2024 audited General Fund balance was $41.95M, including only $4.85M unassigned. The 2027 proposal projects a narrow $1.26M annual surplus and its opening-balance assumptions include $22M of anticipated financing and asset sales. [S1,S2]

Independent educational public-source review, not a certified audit or government finding. Evidence status and proposals are distinguished below; missing information does not prevent publication of available findings.

Findings and processing · Unresolved questions · Original sources · Download structured packets

1. Historical audited position

At December 31, 2024: General Fund total $41,950,453; nonspendable $2,038,078; restricted $18,166,573; committed $10,000,000; assigned $6,893,451; unassigned $4,852,351. The five sum exactly. Unassigned is 11.57% of total, not a cash balance. [S1 p17]

2. Transfers mask the underlying sensitivity

2024 General Fund fell $2,159,812; management discussion reports a $4.8M transfer from excess marijuana tax funds. Holding all else equal, without that transfer the decrease would be approximately $6.96M. This is a counterfactual, not an allegation about legality. [S1]

3. Budget basis is different

December 2024 GAAP fund balance and October 2026 budgetary projections are not directly subtractable. Before comparing, reconcile transfers, investment valuation and reporting basis; do not label the difference cash lost. [S1 pp17,104; S2 p9]

4. 2027 all-funds proposal

The October presentation compares $255.83M 2026 spending with $217.28M 2027 preliminary spending, a $38.55M difference; the General Fund shown in that table is before recommended reductions. This is not realized savings. [S2 p2]

5. 2027 General Fund math

Proposed revenues plus transfers $122.67M and expenses after cuts $121.40M; difference at displayed precision $1.27M versus reported $1.26M, plausibly rounding. The outlook displays starting expenses $131.21M, reductions $9.81M and spending $121.39M. [S2 p8; S3]

6. Personnel reduction screen

The outlook shows 15%, a $66.96M eligible personnel base and $9.81M reduction. Straight multiplication gives $10.044M, a $234,000 difference. The slide says grant-backed/exempt personnel are excluded and elected offices can meet targets in other categories. Reconcile basis and rounding; not evidence of lost cash. [S3 reduction slide]

7. Distribution

Sheriff $46.33M preliminary, $4.89M cut, $41.44M remaining (10.55%; 49.85% of $9.81M total). DA $6.42M to $5.58M, $0.84M reduction (13.08%); facilities $0.68M, clerk $0.60M, planning $0.49M, IT $0.48M. Dollar targets are not layoff counts. [S2 p6]

8. Financing dependency

Slide 9 explains that the General Fund opening estimate for 2027 incorporates unaudited 2025 and 2026 projections, including $7M anticipated asset sale and $15M anticipated COP. Projected end-2026 $0.90M; if neither arrived and all else held constant, $0.90M − $22M = −$21.10M. Not a forecast or proof of issuance. [S2 p9]

9. Other funds

2026 to 2027 projected endings: Road and Bridge −$5.48M to −$5.10M; Human Services $0 to −$0.50M; E911 $4.23M to $4.91M; Excess Marijuana $0.54M to $0.94M; Desert Hawk Golf $1.29M to $1.56M. Restricted resources cannot be assumed transferable. [S2 p9]

10. Debt and recurring costs

Proposed General Fund debt service $21.35M versus $20.22M 2026; capital $1.11M versus $20.13M. Review COP series, collateral, trustee dates and payments through original financing disclosures; a capital decline is not an equivalent recurring cost saving. [S2 p5; S5]

11. Shock test

Use reported annual surplus $1.26M minus revenue loss and unmet savings. At 1% revenue loss ($1.2267M), remaining margin is $33,300; at 2%, deficit $1.1934M. If 90% of $9.81M savings materializes with no other change, surplus $279,000. These are static scenarios, not forecasts; do not double count missing proceeds in annual and opening-balance calculations.

12. Crossover and child impact

Assess jail obligations, pension funding, wildfire recovery, road maintenance, drought, wastewater and service disruptions together. Reduced household income and access to human services are possible knock-on effects; require outcome data before assigning causal effects.

13. Q processing

Retain native dollar amounts, fund, fiscal period, accounting basis, proposal/adoption/actual stage and cited slide or page. Recalculate with integer cents; flag inconsistent rows; keep raw values and derived values separate. Match counterparties for interfund transfers.

14. EE / RX / event horizons

Public-source first; measure recurring revenue against recurring expenditure before financing. In 2027 monitor actual receipts, savings, debt and fund ending balance. In 2029/2031 test reserve recovery and maintenance backlog. Compare alternatives without assuming that all cuts or asset sales are avoidable.
