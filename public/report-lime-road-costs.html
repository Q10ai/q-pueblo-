Lime Road and St Charles: what a commitment line can—and cannot—tell us

Evidence checked 2026-11-09 · Source check date: 2026-11-09

40-point research coverage · Sources

The April 2026 Half-Cent report lists $7,990,246 for St Charles public improvements under project ED2503. That dated commitment is not a verified total for all Lime Road work, a payment total, or a cost attributable solely to Project Falcon.

What the financial record says

The May 13 Half-Cent recap lists $7,990,246 under project ED2503 for St Charles public improvements. This is a commitment in the larger economic-development fund, not proof of the complete cost of Lime Road or a payment to any particular company.

What connects

Road construction, utilities, future maintenance and private development may involve different agreements and beneficiaries. A shared improvement can support multiple users. Establishing each party's cost requires evidence of the specific scope and funding terms.

What is known today from this record

One dated commitment is established. The full road cost, actual payments, maintenance obligations and company-specific benefits are not established. The Half-Cent report and the road report should be read together without counting the same amount twice.

Summary

The answer: The April 2026 Half-Cent report lists $7,990,246 for St Charles public improvements under project ED2503. That dated commitment is not a verified total for all Lime Road work, a payment total, or a cost attributable solely to Project Falcon.

What it means: The available record supports one commitment line, not a consolidated road-cost or company-benefit conclusion. Q’s useful contribution is a defensible cost boundary and an allocation test that can be disproved by better records.

Practical takeaway: Compare the Half-Cent and Lime Road analyses without adding the shared commitment twice.
