Pueblo County’s budget realities: reconstructing the commissioners’ public explanation

Commissioner-shared graphics describe capital commitments, declining marijuana revenue and rising service costs. Recalculation shows $19.068M lower proposed revenue/sources versus the 2026 budget, with lower grants and zero proposed financing accounting for $17.928M of that net change. A proposed $1.265M surplus is a narrow margin, not a final result.

Independent public-source review; not a certified audit or final budget. Screenshot observations, public source findings and Q proposals are distinct.

Findings and processing · Unresolved questions · Public sources · Structured packet · Ask Q

1. Evidence intake

Nine supplied screenshots include a resident question and overlapping captures of a post attributed to Commissioner Paula McPheeters crediting CAO Robert Choi’s 2027 preliminary budget. Deduplicate repeated graphics; do not add overlapping engagement numbers. The post showed 16 reactions, 4 comments and 15 shares in one capture, not a representative survey. The original post and full preliminary PDF could not be independently retrieved. Figures attributed to screenshots are labeled separately from audited or verified documents.

2. Administration's explanation

The post argues the county faces structural problems: historical capital projects, debt service, sustained marijuana receipts decline and higher recurring costs. These can all be real pressures; historical authorization and current budget management are different responsibilities. The original jail refinancing claim concerns initial financing, not every later amendment or payment. [S1–S5 and supplied graphics]

3. Capital and debt

Supplied capital graphic: governmental capital outlays were $10.9M in 2021, $21.2M in 2022, $36.8M in 2023, $118.85M in 2024; debt payments in 2024 $19.83M. 2024 capital outlays are about 10.9 times 2021. Capital spending, financing principal and current operating expense are distinct. The graphic identifies jail/roadway, Workday and Beulah water as commitments; project-specific award, funding source, contract changes and actuals are not established by totals alone.

4. Marijuana revenue

Supplied graph: 2019 $6.0M, 2020 $13.7M, 2021 $14.3M, 2022 $9.3M, 2023 $7.9M, 2024 $6.6M, 2025 $5.39M (rounded graph $5.4M). It reports a 62.3% peak-to-2025 fall, $8.90M lower revenue and rate change from 3.5% to 6% in 2020. Four consecutive lower receipts following 2021 are a planning warning; no conclusion about individual knowledge or intent follows. These are combined tax streams, not all unrestricted General Fund revenue.

6. Revenue reconciliation

Displayed totals $116,960,406 / $141,734,585 / $122,666,601. Row sum for 2025 is $116,960,405, a $1 difference; 2026 and 2027 reconcile exactly. 2027 is $19,067,984 below 2026 budget; grant decrease $12,427,888 plus financing decrease $5,500,000 = $17,927,888 (94.02% of net decrease). Property decreases $1,896,852; sales rises $18,000. These are proposal-versus-budget changes not actual losses or proof grant funds could pay for eligible personnel.

7. Recurring revenue nuance

2025 recorded total includes $3.686M financing, whereas 2027 proposal has zero financing. Proposed total is $5,706,195 greater than the displayed 2025 recorded total; excluding financing, difference $9,392,195. Grants/transfers may still be temporary or restricted, so these comparisons do not establish a structural operating surplus. Do not compare preliminary 2025 recorded numbers as audited actuals.

8. Six cost drivers

Supplied graphic, 2023 actual / 2025 recorded: property/liability insurance $967,345 / $1,658,262; detention medical $4,656,465 / $5,894,700; health claims/admin $13,404,845 / $15,879,224; utilities $2,410,939 / $2,674,738; repairs/maintenance contracts $395,586 / $1,017,949; detention overtime $1,779,099 / $2,445,239. Total $23,614,279 to $29,570,112 = $5,955,833 increase (25.22%).

9. Detention crossover

Medical increase $1,238,235 (26.59%) plus overtime increase $666,140 (37.44%) = $1,904,375, or about 32% of the selected cost rise. These are gross cost areas, not the whole sheriff budget or verified causal effects of the new jail. Health claims/admin is shown before reimbursements. Connect staffing, custody demand, city–county agreements, contracts and health coverage; do not count these increases twice.

10. Budget exact arithmetic

Supplied graphic lists revenue/sources $122,666,601; base expenditures $131,213,309; reported base shortfall $8,546,709; reductions $9,811,472; proposed expenditures $121,401,837; reported surplus $1,264,763. Recalculation gives shortfall $8,546,708, expenses $121,401,837, surplus $1,264,764. Two one-dollar differences likely underlying precision/rounding; not evidence of lost money. Reported surplus is 1.031% of proposed revenue.

11. Margin stress test

Holding other items constant, 1% revenue loss $1,226,666 leaves about $38,097 of reported surplus. 90% attainment of the $9,811,472 reduction gives $283,616. Budget-balance headroom is not evidence that reserve targets or services are adequately funded. Do not combine annual shock with the separately projected opening balance.

12. Opening balance and fiscal policy

October slide 9 expressly says the opening balance incorporates $7M anticipated asset sale and $15M anticipated COP. No financing in the 2027 annual revenue table does not contradict possible 2026 financing. Separate years and verify transactions. County progress report says policy sets 10% minimum and 16.67% target; subsequent schedule and exact eligible reserve measure need confirmation. [S2,S4]

13. Inherited versus current governance

Assign each project original authorization, later amendments, new financing, approved budget, expenditure, forecast and operating handoff to independently dated resolutions. The 2024 audit documents lease obligations but alone does not establish a complete responsibility matrix. Do not accuse a named official or exonerate all current decisions solely from the graphic. [S3]

14. Options and child impact

Compare recurring-cost controls, contract renewal, job vacancy treatment, debt and capital plans, restricted grant continuity and public safety service assessment. Evaluate prevention, detention healthcare, staffing, child reporting/education services and families receiving grants. No confirmed equivalent savings or causal harms claimed.

15. Q processing, LL, EE, T2Y and shadow

Record screenshot-derived rows as source observations, not final audit figures; retain original display totals and recalculated sums. Version the November communications, match the original budget and audit when available, never overwrite historical figures silently. Reuse data across pension, public safety, jail, Desert Hawk and regional packets; separate staff time saved from cash savings.

16. RX, procurement and leap

Require project authorization packets with 10–30 year debt, operations, staffing, maintenance, revenue sensitivities, contract commitments and exit plan; review Workday interoperability before new technology spending. A full-cost capital plan and monthly exception dashboard can help prevent reconstruction. Test read-only on historical figures; human authorization required.

17. Event horizons

Before final 2027 budget adoption, update figures and alternatives; publish actual-vs-budget plus debt and grant reconciliation monthly in 2027. Compare reserve progress, recurring income and service outcomes through 2029 and 2031. No claim these practices are fully implemented. [S4]
