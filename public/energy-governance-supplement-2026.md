# Who controls Pueblo’s power? — Energy governance and mutual aid supplement

**QP-ENERGY-2026-0232E-SUP-01 | November 19, 2026 | Editorial review required | Non-operational**

Parent: QP-ENERGY-2026-0232E v2.0.0. Review due November 25 or material event.

## 1. Scope and finding

This supplement extends QP-ENERGY-2026-0232E v2.0.0, not a competing investigation. It examines compensation, household exposure, ownership alternatives and regional development. The four submitted social screenshots are qualitative signals only: concerns about wage ratios, failed separation efforts, local solar access, voluntary conservation and government credibility. No account identities, screenshots, quotations or representative survey claims are published. The rate case is pending; neither misconduct nor a viable acquisition has been established.

## 2. Compensation: compare like with like

The 2026 proxy reports 2025 former parent CEO Linden Evans salary $995,594 and total compensation $5,991,759, including $2,966,688 stock awards, $1,294,886 non-equity incentives, $4,179 pension-value change and $730,412 other compensation. The reported company median total is $129,748; the issuer's ratio is 46:1. Grant-date compensation is not cash received. At Colorado's 2026 $15.16 hourly wage and 2,080 paid hours, gross annual wages are $31,532.80; the historical CEO total is 190.0 times that cross-year benchmark. The 2024 total of $5,787,141 divided by that benchmark is 183.5, a possible explanation for the circulating 183 figure, not proof of its origin. A same-year 2025 minimum wage of $14.81 produces $30,804.80 and a ratio of roughly 194.5. The statutory presidential salary is $400,000, excluding a $50,000 expense allowance. It is a public-service comparator, not a legal private-sector ceiling. The utility's actual lowest worker compensation and Colorado recoverable share remain unknown. [S01,S03,S04]

## 4. Ratepayer exposure and lawful policy design

The proposal seeks $26,663,479 additional annual revenue; $124.18 baseline, $135.08 with credit, $142.48 without at 600 kWh. Annual increases are $130.80 and $219.60. Parent pay cannot be directly assigned to customers; inspect allocated overhead, salaries, incentive treatments, merger fees, severance, tax and accounting adjustments, disallowances and effective orders. Colorado SB23-291 already limits recovery of specified expenses, including board compensation above 50%, certain lobbying, investor relations and travel; it does not establish a universal CEO 20:1 cap. A potential policy is a recoverable-compensation ceiling or disclosure standard, subject to statutory authority, evidentiary process and legal review. A broader pay ratio must define total compensation consistently for workers and executives, full-time equivalents, contractors, geographic scope, benefits and deferred equity; prevent outsourcing evasion and consider recruitment, service reliability and cost shifting. [S05–S07]

## 5. 20:1 sensitivity, not a savings forecast

Illustrative wage-based ceiling is $630,656, $230,656 above statutory presidential salary. The difference from historical CEO total is $5,361,103. Let a be the fraction of that difference actually recoverable in Colorado rates and p the fraction passed through: hypothetical relief = $5,361,103 × a × p. At a=0 relief is zero; at a=0.1 and p=1 relief is $536,110.30 annually; 100% allocation/pass-through yields $5,361,103, a deliberately extreme mechanical bound rather than realistic savings. No allocation factor has been established. Uniform division over 102,000 accounts is not valid actual class-rate design. Compare compensation reform with other expense and return-on-equity decisions in the master paper.

## 6. Community conservation and safety

A household avoiding 100 W of decorative lighting for six hours daily over 30 days saves 18 kWh. At an assumed marginal rate of $0.18/kWh, gross avoided charges are $3.24; 10,000 participating households would avoid 180 MWh and $32,400. This is not utility profit lost, a tariff quote, or a claim of expected participation. Fixed charges remain. Voluntary conservation must never involve nonpayment, service tampering, coordinated reconnection of large loads, or disabling heating, medical devices, refrigeration, accessibility or safety lighting.

## 7. Local generation: capacity versus entitlement

Bighorn is 300 MW DC / 240 MW AC; Sun Mountain 293 MW DC / 200 MW AC. Combined capacity is 593 MW DC / 440 MW AC, not firm dispatchable power or annual energy. The developer reports Xcel PPAs and Bighorn's relationship to the steel mill. Local generation does not confer a household right to low-cost electricity in a different utility service territory. Recover PPA eligibility, transmission, capacity accreditation, balancing, curtailment, interconnection and distribution rights; do not publish restricted grid information. [S09,S10]

## 8. Municipalization and 2030 decision gates

The 2025 GDS/BBK city-commissioned draft estimated $383–503M acquisition-related costs in 2030 dollars and found city-only acquisition infeasible under its assumptions. Its table's line-item ranges are not simply additive across scenarios. A separate utility-sponsored study is retained in the master packet; do not mix financing scope and acquisition values. The 2020 and 2025 vote history needs certified counts and independent campaign finance research; distrust and poor planning are hypotheses, not established motives. 2030 is a planning horizon, not a guarantee of any election or automatic franchise exit. Test retained service and reforms, community solar, cooperative or joint authority, third-party acquisition and municipal utility on equal boundaries and reliability. [S08,S15]

## 9. Ownership financing and no-go criteria

Illustratively, $400M financed over 30 years at 4%, 6% or 8% produces annual debt service of about $23.13M, $29.06M or $35.53M before operating and energy costs. Require independent asset valuation, legally available firm power, interconnection, reserve adequacy, bond terms and stress tests, competent staffing, outage mutual aid, emergency procedures, billing and consumer protections. Compare annual all-in bills by class and risk-adjusted lifecycle cost; test lower sales, delayed acquisition, higher interest and fuel prices. No acquisition without an independent review, public disclosure, authorized decision and continuity plan.

No collection or enrollment is enabled. Before launch require an authorized fiscal sponsor, consent, secure intake, conflict disclosures, eligibility and appeal rules, vendor verification, independent reconciliation and accessible multilingual navigation. Never expose addresses, accounts, medical status or donor-recipient links. [S11–S14,S20]

## 11. Regional jobs, AI and public finance

Industrial energy needs must be compared with residential protection. For any data center, manufacturing or Comanche redevelopment idea, require an actual proposal, peak and annual load, water/cooling design, firm supply, connection and upgrade allocations, tax and economic incentive terms, employment quality, lifecycle emissions, stranded asset security and emergency demand plans. The separate resource plan and economic-development rates are distinct cases in the master packet. No named AI project is treated as confirmed here. Crosswalk municipal and county energy spending to budgets, nonprofit service continuity, housing, emergency readiness and workforce; do not infer budget mismanagement from social comments.

## 12. Implementation and accountability roadmap

2026: household referrals, public hearing information, obtain official rate docket, pay allocation and original election/franchise documents. 2027: independent city/regional feasibility procurement and matched tariff study, conflict review and public cost model. 2028: opt-in solar, efficiency and resilience pilots with measured savings and reliability. 2029: decision-ready comparison, funding and operations tests. 2030: lawful decision only if gates pass. Proposed owners are roles, not commitments: regulator/UCA for rate case, civic records custodians for budgets, qualified engineers and counsel for feasibility, nonprofits/fiscal sponsor for assistance, residents for voluntary participation. Report progress as evidence arrives, not as a falsely completed program.
