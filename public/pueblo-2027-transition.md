## 11. City 2027 budget
November 14 secondary comparison reports proposed General Fund spending about $125m, projected ending balance $2.2m, zero pay raise and frozen positions. Pending final vote, not audited actuals. [B,C]

## 12. County 2027 budget
Published proposal and dashboard: $9.81m proposed reductions, 15% eligible General Fund personnel base rather than every entire department. Assess unrestricted cash independently. [D,F,B]

## 13. Audit and disclosures
County dashboard reports corrective accounting work; final audit and payroll liquidity require original records. Preserve separate disclosure, compensation and audit investigations. [F]

## 14. Council attorney funding
Professional services appropriation is not spendable cash. Obtain a purpose-level amendment/commitment reconciliation before relying on independent counsel funding. See dedicated article. [C]

## 15. Sheriff labor and staffing
No verified final layoff count. Obtain executed collective agreement, funded/filled positions and safe service floors; compare net savings after backfill, overtime and separation.

## 16. Jail and municipal policy
November 23 detention vote is reported in the earlier investigation. Require final ordinance, signed city/county agreement, safely staffed capacity, actual incremental costs and applicable sentencing limits before implementation. [G,D]

## 17. Schools and child safety
Preserve separate District 70 SRO investigation. No verified program cut; request joint funding and continuity plan and aggregate outcomes. Never disclose school-specific vulnerabilities.

## 18. Health marketplace
Enrollment through Jan 15; select by Dec 15 for Jan 1 coverage, Dec 16–Jan 15 for Feb 1 coverage. Use certified assistance and confirm plan networks and net premiums. [I]

## 19. Medicaid changes
Jan 1 applicable work requirements and more frequent eligibility checks are scheduled; affected March renewal members receive notices in January. Official guidance includes 80 hours approved activities or $580 earnings for covered persons, and exemptions. Do not assume all members are affected. [M,H,Q]

## 20. Medicare and Social Security
SSA announces 3.6% COLA; increased SSI paid Dec 31, other benefits starting January. Gross increase is not net deposit after deductions. Check individual notice and Medicare plan information. [S,MED]

## 21. Employment and wages
Colorado employers should verify 2027 minimum wage and applicable local/federal requirements before first payroll. Exact new wage not established in this review; do not invent a rate. [LAB]

## 22. FAMLI payroll
State employer bulletin sets total 2027 FAMLI premium at 0.86% of wages, down from 0.88%, effective Jan 1 for covered employers. October briefing identifies new tax obligations for certain medical leave benefits and employers with ten or more staff. Check employer scope. [W,Y]

## 23. Food, housing and heating
LEAP heating assistance season runs Nov 1–Apr 30; eligibility and benefits vary. Refer households to official benefits and 211 resources, not a promise of awards. [Z,HUM,HELP]

## 24. Electricity rate proposals
Black Hills June filing proposes average $10.90/month (8.8%) residential base-rate increase, March 2027 if approved. Separate DSMCA filing proposes Jan 1 $0.28/month average residential increase. These are proposals, not final tariffs; do not add to individual bill as certainty. [U,J,AG]

## 25. Water, sewer and solid waste
Verify utility-specific 2027 rate resolutions, service areas and billing schedules. No verified 2027 local water/sewer tariff or universal change found in this pass. Do not apply city rates to other districts.

## 26. Roads and infrastructure
Nov 16 reporting describes about $1.75m half-cent funding for south Pueblo Boulevard/Mirror improvements, temporary to permanent signal, and CDOT projects by I-25. Distinguish project approval from awarded construction, completion and reimbursement. [V]

## 27. TIF and neighborhood investment
Track half-cent reimbursement, certified increment, agreement, project milestones and maintenance. North Elizabeth parcel plan is not corridor-wide approval. See independent TIF and Elizabeth reports.

## 28. Economic development and Ecoasis
Nov 16 public meeting described proposed greenhouse campus and integrated data center; developers project 1,000+ jobs and food production. Neither permits, hiring nor power/water capacity established. Obtain land-use application, contracts, resource budgets and development conditions. [X]

## 29. Disaster recovery
County dashboard lists 453 households receiving temporary lodging, 36 dumpsters, 81 state requests, $321,833.63 identified county costs (not comprehensive incident cost). County reports Nov 5 declaration renewal. Rebuilding guidance updated Oct 19; verify current individual assistance/appeal status before advising. [R,K,F]

## 30. Rail and special districts
Nov 13 Pueblo County unofficial 7A results: 20,599 yes/18,114 no; Rye 7B 1,540 yes/1,002 no; Edison 6D 96 for/39 against. Rail district totals and certification govern districtwide outcome. 7A ballot text proposes 0.333% sales/use tax; effective date and service opening not verified. [P,RR]

## 31. New state ballot measures
Secondary reports list passed/failing measures; final statutory text, certification, implementation dates and applicable scope require separate review before treating any rule as operative. [N]

## 32. AI governance
Attorney General states SB26-189 and HB26-1263 effective Jan 1. Review consequential decision uses, data correction rights, scope and final rules; prohibit automatic Q decisions about rights, employment, benefits or policing. [A]

## 33. Resident actions now
Check insurance deadlines, benefit notices, heat assistance, public agendas and official source links; affected fire households should use current rebuilding guidance. No personal information should be submitted to Q. [I,H,Z,K,G]

## 34. LL — lessons from real practice
GFOA rolling cash flow forecasts give incoming teams a common cash baseline; Colorado's marketplace enrollment schedule demonstrates action-specific deadlines; the county fire dashboard provides a local recovery ledger pattern. Adapt, do not equate status reports with audited outcomes. [LL,I,R]

## 35. RX — leadership handover
Propose a joint written handover: adopted budgets, unencumbered cash, contracts, debt, open litigation, grants, staffing/service floors, overdue records, capital obligations and unresolved public questions. Each accountable office signs its factual scope, not a collective political judgment.

## 36. RX — everyday service continuity
Maintain accessible public notices and referrals; compare changes in wait times, coverage, school support and adverse outcomes. Preserve alternatives in separate reports; never recommend an unsupported layoff or service shutdown.

## 37. EE and T2Y
Reuse existing public exports and established data definitions. Track actual cost, staff time, coverage and outcomes over 30/90 days and one year. No speculative AI savings are included as funding.

## 38. Twin and Shadow
Store source date, observation date, authority, status and next review separately. Community comments remain observation signals; model conditional budget or service shocks without overwriting official state. Protect identities, case details and operational security.

## 39. Event horizons and LEAP
Review after Nov 23/Dec 10 votes, Jan 1 legal/tariff changes, Jan 11/12 transitions and Jan 15 checkpoint. Later watch March rate/Medicaid implementation, April 30 LEAP season end, October Medicaid funding changes. No automated monitor currently enabled. [B,A,H,U,Q,Z]

## 40. Qified synthesis and outstanding work
Priority is verified authority and continuity through handover, not a prediction of political behavior. Obtain certified election results, adopted budgets, final state ballot provisions, tariffs, departmental staffing plans, project approvals, current benefit guidance and local school/special district calendars. This is a broad dated first edition, not an exhaustive inventory of every local change.
