🍇 The Rumor Mill

Bring the story; let's see what the evidence can support. Vent privately, sort fact from feeling and have a non-sensitive public claim considered without publishing an accusation.

Every claim is eligible for scrutiny; not every rumor contains truth. The aim is to acknowledge someone's experience, investigate a public proposition fairly, and avoid amplifying unsupported allegations. This is not a public rumor feed or protected whistleblower service.

📮 Submit a public claim for private human consideration

Have an old Pueblo story, historical claim, misunderstood date, public-policy question or attributable public-source correction? Use the established Q-Pueblo email route. The website does not collect or display your message. You choose whether to send it in your own email app.

✉️ Email a public story for considerationCorrections and sourcesKeep it private: reflection sheet
Privacy and reporting boundary: Email is not anonymous or confidential, may be retained by email providers, and no response or investigation is guaranteed. Never include identifiable private-person allegations, child information, sensitive medical details, confidential evidence, or threats. Urgent safety, abuse, criminal or legally protected disclosures belong with appropriate competent authorities or protected channels. No submission is published automatically.

What happens to a reviewable submission?

  1. Receive and screen: a human may examine the email, exclude sensitive personal claims or unsafe material.
  2. Classify: separate first-hand experience, folklore, official statement, hearsay, value and factual hypothesis.
  3. Trace: seek the original public record, scope, date, independent corroboration and contradiction.
  4. Explain a bounded result: supported, partly supported, contradicted, unresolved or outside scope; none proves a person's motive.
  5. Human publication gate: an independent, privacy-safe educational explanation may be published after review—not the original message or an unverified accusation.
  6. Correct and amend: preserve previous versions when new evidence changes the result.

⚙️ Test familiar claims and assumptions

The grapevine🍇 Everybody says it, but who said it first?

“Everybody knows it happened that way.”

Put it through the mill
  • Who first reported it, and on what date?
  • Is this a direct witness account, official record, inference, or repeated quote?
  • What independent source would change the conclusion?

What survives: Frequency of repetition is not independent corroboration. Look for the original record and independent confirmation.

State: SOURCE NOT YET IDENTIFIED

A history mix-up⏳ The Riverwalk opened in 1921, didn't it?

“I heard Pueblo's Riverwalk was built in 1921.”

Put it through the mill
  • Could two historical events be mixed together?
  • Is there an original Riverwalk timeline?
  • What happened in 1921 instead?

What survives: The major Pueblo flood occurred in 1921; the modern Historic Arkansas Riverwalk opened in 1999. A confident date can still attach to the wrong event.

State: DOCUMENTED CORRECTION

Money maze💰 They announced a grant. So the cash is gone?

“The project was promised money, so the full amount must have been spent.”

Put it through the mill
  • Is it proposed, authorized, appropriated, contracted or disbursed?
  • Where is the controlling fund and payment ledger?
  • What performance conditions and recoveries apply?

What survives: An announcement or appropriation does not establish disbursement or results. Different financial stages need separate evidence.

State: HYPOTHESIS — REVIEW THE LEDGER

Motive trap🎭 They disagree, so someone must have a hidden agenda?

“If two groups disagree, one of them must be dishonest.”

Put it through the mill
  • Can both parties have different legitimate interests?
  • Which statements are publicly attributable?
  • What decision standard or falsifiable proposition would help?

What survives: Disagreement may concern values, goals, tradeoffs, facts, or different exposure to risk. Motive is not established by disagreement alone.

State: NOT AN EVIDENCE-BASED INFERENCE

Feelings versus claims💬 I'm angry. Does that mean I need to prove my feelings?

“This decision makes me worried and frustrated.”

Put it through the mill
  • What part is a feeling or lived experience?
  • What concrete outcome or change caused concern?
  • What could be checked without attacking anyone?

What survives: The feeling can be acknowledged without requiring proof of emotion. A factual claim about cause, law, action or harm still needs evidence.

State: EXPERIENCE IS REAL; CAUSE MAY REMAIN UNKNOWN

Tech talk🤖 AI said it with confidence. Isn't that enough?

“The chatbot showed three citations, so every line is verified.”

Put it through the mill
  • Do the cited pages actually say what the answer claims?
  • Are the dates, geography, and jurisdiction correct?
  • Could citations be copied, fabricated, or irrelevant?

What survives: AI confidence, citation formatting, and repeated summaries do not independently verify a proposition.

State: CHECK THE CLAIM, NOT THE TONE

Power and accountability🏛️ A name appears beside an issue—who's accountable?

“That organization was tagged with an issue, so it controls the decision.”

Put it through the mill
  • What is the legal authority and time period?
  • Who proposes, authorizes, pays, operates, regulates, and reviews?
  • Which roles are directly documented versus listed as possible stakeholders?

What survives: A source-index association is not proof of power, funding, negligence, wrongdoing, or an official's responsibility.

State: ROLE NEEDS AN ORIGINAL RECORD

Future claims🔭 Someone predicts a disaster. Is it already happening?

“A scenario model shows a risk, so the outcome is certain.”

Put it through the mill
  • What inputs and assumptions were used?
  • How does it perform against past observations?
  • What would make the prediction wrong?

What survives: A plausible scenario is not an observed result or a certainty. Compare baselines, uncertainty and model validation.

State: SCENARIO — NOT A VERIFIED OUTCOME

💬 When people just need to vent

A genuine feeling requires no proof. An explanation of what caused it, or an accusation about someone else, does require evidence. You can recognize someone’s frustration without adjudicating facts or turning a complaint into a public rumor. Use the offline worksheet to decide whether you want to be heard, learn, correct, appeal, seek support or take no action.
