Pueblo Boulevard: reconciling the funding listed on the October 26 agenda

Evidence checked 2026-11-10 · Check the listed review date.

Findings and more detail · Sources

Short answer

The October 26, 2026 Council agenda lists proposed funding sources totaling $2,058,277.00 for road and signal work, matching its listed $1,658,277.00 construction contract plus $400,000.00 contingency. This is an arithmetic reconciliation of agenda amounts, not evidence of adoption, execution, payment or final cost. The adjacent PURA reimbursement item needs its own agreement.

The full story

What was proposed

On October 26, the Council agenda listed a $1,658,277 construction contract and $400,000 contingency for Pueblo Boulevard road and signal work. Four listed funding sources totaled $2,058,277: $1,756,383.40 in Half-Cent funds, two signal deposits of $32,561.60 and $19,332, and a $250,000 Martinez contribution.

What connects

The proposed sources and uses balance exactly. A neighboring agenda item describes a City–PURA reimbursement agreement for improvements between Acero and Mirror. Without its actual terms, adding that agreement to the construction amount risks double counting.

What the agenda establishes

The October agenda documents proposed funding and a named contract, not the later vote, payment or acceptance of completed work. This story preserves that dated scope instead of presenting a proposal as final delivery.

The agenda supports a precise planning reconciliation while leaving the legal and delivery states open.

What the available evidence cannot establish

  • A balanced list of proposed sources and uses does not establish that a project is fully funded in cash, that costs are final, or that the PURA agreement covers the entire amount. Later amendments could change this baseline.
  • Final adopted ordinance, amendments, roll-call vote, executed contract, reimbursement terms and actual payments are not established here.
  • Whether contingency was used, the final allocation between accounts, maintenance responsibility and accepted construction outcomes require later records.
