One evidence base. Different ways to use it.

Residents & families

Find public records, understand agencies and services, examine household costs and impacts on children, and prepare questions for decision-makers.

Business & investment

Organize due diligence on permits, infrastructure, funding, workforce, contracts, risk and lifecycle obligations. Independently verify current requirements.

Government & public workers

Trace public authority, budget stages, meeting decisions, implementation responsibilities, alternatives and potential service impacts.

Researchers & educators

Compare sources, preserve history and contradictions, learn about Pueblo institutions and prepare reproducible research questions.

Every question deserves a useful next step.

  1. 5Ws, how and how much: who, what, when, where, why, how, funding stage, jurisdiction and lawful responsibility.
  2. Evidence and counterevidence: distinguish official records, attributed explanations, independent analysis, community signals and unknowns.
  3. Connections and consequences: inspect direct, secondary and third-order relationships only where documented; speculative causal links stay labeled.
  4. Children and the future: consider present services, inherited costs, educational opportunity, safety, privacy and future scenarios; mark impacts unknown if evidence is missing.
  5. Options and recovery: prepare records requests, meeting questions, comparisons and response plans without pretending Q-Pueblo can issue permits, dispatch help or decide policy.

When a topic has not been researched, the workbench can still organize a source-search plan, a records-request template and a question list. The browser-only tool does not independently verify an answer or contact institutions.

SHOCK, LEAP and RX: preparedness without prediction claims

SHOCK · Test disruptions

Ask what an economic, flood, wildfire, utility, funding, cyber or institutional disruption could affect. Models are conditional exercises, not forecasts.

LEAP · Explore alternatives

Compare feasible responses, resource demands, uncertainty, reversibility, and child/family implications. People decide what to implement.

RX · Maintain continuity

Look for substitute sources, contact pathways, corrections, backups and recovery steps. A missing record never becomes an invented fact.

Start with a real Pueblo question

Choose an issue, read the source limitations, open primary records, then use the workbench to form follow-up questions or a review-ready packet. If you identify an error, use the site's correction guidance and cite the exact dated statement or document; do not post confidential data or private details about children.
