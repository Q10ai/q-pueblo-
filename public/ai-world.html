Governance and rights

Ask who authorizes a system, what it decides, what evidence is used, how it is reviewed and how mistakes can be corrected. Colorado's Attorney General describes enacted automated decision-making and chatbot safety laws scheduled to take effect January 1, 2027. Applicability and current rules require verification; do not assume every tool is covered. Colorado AI rulemaking · NIST risk management

Actors and influence

Study public institutions, technology vendors, regulators, researchers, workers and investors by documented roles, authority, conduct and results. Separate known beneficial or harmful conduct from allegations and unknown attribution. Never infer a person's motives from an association. No private resident profiles are needed.

Institution directory · Follow public money

Business and industry

Compare actual tasks and outcomes with manual alternatives. Include licensing, training, supervision, error correction, infrastructure and supplier exit. Consult workers and plan for changed skills without assuming job loss. Choosing not to adopt a tool can be the best decision.

Healthcare and human services

AI can support administrative work and clinical research. Verify the intended use and evidence before applying it to care. Preserve licensed professional authority, patient privacy, accessibility, appeals and manual alternatives. Do not rely on an AI output for an emergency or individualized medical decision.

Infrastructure and resources

Assess energy, water, connectivity, maintenance, cybersecurity and dependence on suppliers. Use documented capacity and demand before estimating local impacts. Distinguish a proposed facility from approved, built and operating facilities.
