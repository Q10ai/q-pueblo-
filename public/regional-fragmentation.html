One Pueblo region, many disconnected reporting systems

Public budgets and financial disclosures are maintained by separate institutions. PACOG already provides a regional coordination structure; Q proposes federated public information, not consolidated government. [S1–S5]

Independent educational public-source review, not a certified audit or government finding. Evidence status and proposals are distinguished below; missing information does not prevent publication of available findings.

Findings and processing · Unresolved questions · Original sources · Download structured packets

1. Scope

One regional economy intersects city and county boundaries, metro and special districts, schools, water, sanitation, libraries, emergency response, nonprofits, businesses and state/federal programs. These authorities retain different statutory mandates and records.

2. Existing public reporting

County, city, Pueblo West, Pueblo Water and schools have distinct sources. State filings offer a separate oversight channel. Differences in fiscal years and accounting bases prevent direct comparisons without reconciliation. [S2–S8]

3. An existing coordinating body

PACOG lists regional member government organizations and provides a forum for coordination. Q proposes exploring its convening role for compatibility; it has not been granted additional authority or adopted this design. [S1]

4. Why the fragmentation costs matter

Residents search multiple portals; entities may buy overlapping data, maps or technology; cross-jurisdictional dependencies are difficult to identify. These are hypotheses requiring time-and-cost baselines, not audited dollar losses.

5. Follow the money

A federal grant passed to the state, county and a local service provider can be recorded more than once. Match transfer amount, date, fund, counterparty and recognition basis. Sum comparable expenditures minus only matched duplicate internal flows; preserve unresolved matches.

6. Illustrative regional case

When county road maintenance is deferred, municipal and school transport patterns may change; emergency access and local business costs may be affected. Track service boundaries and jointly affected assets without asserting effects are already measured.

7. Water and industry

Link water rights, allocations, drought, wastewater, projects, industrial demand and utility capacity. Distinguish legal rights, physical water, seasonal availability and contracted use; avoid drawing conclusions from unrelated service territories.

8. Fire and emergency

Use verified event dates, recovery milestones, household assistance, grant and asset packets. Link each cost to the responsible entity and do not count promised assistance as paid. Year 3/5 recovery monitoring belongs alongside immediate response.

9. Education and household impact

Schools use different fiscal calendars. Child effects of transport, food availability, household income and service changes should be modeled in aggregate, without profiling children.

10. Digital twin and shadow

Twin: source-backed public facts about institutions, assets, responsibilities and connections. Shadow: historical versions, observed public concerns and scenarios; distinguish source-backed states from hypothetical outcomes. Neither controls physical infrastructure.

11. Public signals

Community comments are retained as dated thematic observations, not population sentiment or verified wrongdoing. Link them to claims and relevant sources; avoid publishing unnecessary identifiers. Preserve contradictory viewpoints.

12. EE / T2Y / LL

Measure duplicated research time and data acquisition; shared definitions and stable identifiers can reduce rework. Maintain time validity and versions, preserve dissent and recover existing Q evidence before new extraction.

13. RX and leap

Pilot common exports with willing partners and existing public archives. Use a read-only regional source registry and compatibility adapter rather than a centralized replacement. Human authority, local autonomy, legal retention and privacy remain intact.

14. Horizon and success

2027 inventory and pilots; 2029 reconciled financial flows and GIS relationships; 2031 independent resilience evaluation. Success means fewer unresolved matches, reliable publication and proven reduction in rework — not number of entities forced onto one platform.

What remains unresolved

  • No complete current entity and service-boundary census.
  • No measured region-wide fragmentation cost.
  • Institutional adoption, agreements and legal scope not established.

Q build and processing output

Federated regional source inventory and entity relationship schema; no new authority or institutional consolidation.

Available now: static article, structured export, source citations, connected research and dated review status. Proposed models and future integrations are not live services.
