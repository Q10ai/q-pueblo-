Rye and Beulah: mitigation, burn-scar watersheds and the next storm

Recovery requires protective works and maintenance, not just rebuilding.

Why the second shock matters

Fire can alter vegetation, runoff, erosion and sediment movement. The severity varies by site, rainfall, terrain and soil; Q cannot clear a parcel or forecast a particular flood. Rebuilding and mitigation have to be considered together with professional site evaluation.

What a recovery program can cover

NRCS EWP works through eligible sponsors and may cover qualified watershed measures such as erosion protection and debris removal under signed agreements. It does not fund everything: ordinary maintenance and certain utility or transportation reconstruction are excluded. Confirm the specific project agreement and eligibility before spending. Agricultural producers can consult NRCS disaster recovery programs; eligibility is not assumed.

Who and what to ask

County parks/natural resources, Road and Bridge, purchasing and finance; Colorado State Forest Service; relevant fire districts and forest managers; conservation districts, NRCS, utilities, affected property owners and residents. Verify actual participation from project records. Request treatment polygons, prescription, award, change orders, invoices, inspection and recurring maintenance owner. Do not treat a plan as completed acreage or infer that prior treatments would have prevented the fire.

Financial and child crossovers

Compare accepted project cost, documented acres or structures protected and funded upkeep. Account for family access, school routes, rural isolation, water quality, wildlife and recreational access. Keep hazard data and vulnerable households protected.

LEAP milestones

2027 review completed work and post-treatment condition; 2029 compare flood/erosion experience and maintenance status; 2031 assess lifecycle effectiveness and replace/maintain according to engineering and forestry review. Event horizons: official flood warnings, failed access or water supply, no maintenance budget. Use official alerts, not Q, for current action.

Q processing, forensics and scenario

Treatment coverage = verified effective treated area / verified area requiring treatment. Compare actual contract and approved changes to accepted scope, then evaluate erosion and runoff outcomes. Avoid implying prior work would have prevented the fire.
