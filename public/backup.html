BACKUP: What if things stop working?

Continuity planning from individuals to governments. Review candidate; not an official emergency plan, live response system or authorization to operate critical infrastructure. Follow official instructions during emergencies.

Plan A through safe stop

  1. Normal operations
  2. Independent alternate
  3. Local or offline service
  4. Trained manual procedures
  5. Authorized mutual aid and recovery
  6. Safe stop for unsafe functions, while responsible people protect essential services.

Find your situation

Households and people

Potential failures: Power, internet, device loss, food costs, displacement

Options: Printed contacts and health information; safe household supplies; independent communications; trusted-person check-ins; official shelter and assistance routes.

AI and software

Potential failures: Model outage, false answers, bad updates, API lockout, compromised memory

Options: Freeze consequential automation; use verified prior evidence with freshness labels; independent approved provider; local deterministic tools; human review and manual procedures.

Schools and childcare

Potential failures: Learning platform failure, cyber incident, staffing or building loss

Options: Printed learning materials; teacher-led offline instruction; safe reunification procedures; alternate approved venues; protect student records.

Healthcare

Potential failures: Record system outage, staffing, medication or utility disruption

Options: Use approved clinical downtime procedures; paper records with reconciliation; verified medication records; authorized mutual aid; never improvise clinical care.

Businesses and suppliers

Potential failures: Payment outages, supplier delay, fuel shocks, lost data

Options: Preapproved alternate suppliers; offline transaction procedures with reconciliation; cross-trained staff; cash-flow stress tests; verified restore points.

Utilities and infrastructure

Potential failures: Electricity, water, communications, transport and shared dependency failure

Options: Authorized continuity and emergency plans; independent power and communications where engineered; priority essential services and verified restoration. No amateur operational switching.

Local and regional government

Potential failures: Office closure, cyberattack, revenue loss, staff shortage

Options: Essential function inventory; lawful succession and delegations; alternate sites; accessible communication; mutual aid agreements; continuity cost and deferred-work ledgers.

State, national and cross-border systems

Potential failures: Compound infrastructure failure, fiscal shock, supply disruption

Options: Jurisdiction-specific continuity plans; lawful mutual aid; independent supply and communications paths; civilian rights protection; coordinated recovery. No central Q command authority.

Q10 and Knowledge OS

Potential failures: Loss of provider, device, operator, records, access or trust

Options: Versioned exports, provider-separated and offline encrypted copies, independently tested restoration, successor authority, secret rotation, quarantine, verified rollback and human reactivation.

Try a backup failure exercise

Hypothetical demonstration: two alternates share different dependencies. A working copy is not proof of restoration. Select a failed dependency, change your recovery limits and mark only the exercise conditions you intend to simulate. Nothing is stored, sent or activated.

Shared cloud alternate

Uses cloud A and internet A; hypothetical recovery 1 hour, data loss 0 hours.

Local offline alternate

Uses local power and trained staff; hypothetical recovery 2 hours, data loss 1 hour.

Complete the exercise to compare options.

For real continuity decisions, consult the responsible operator. This exercise is not a restoration test, a live emergency tool or authority to switch infrastructure.

Before a failure

Identify essential services, their owners, minimum service, maximum tolerable outage and data loss, common dependencies, recovery points, lawful authority, independent alternatives, operating costs and triggers. Test restoration; do not mistake a successful copy for a recoverable system.

During and after

Protect life first. Isolate compromised systems when authorized, stop unsafe automation, verify alternative capacity and authority, maintain evidence, reconcile manual transactions, restore in dependency order and validate before human-authorized reactivation. Review the incident and amend the plan.

Build your offline worksheet

Print this page and write the answers privately. Do not publish sensitive infrastructure or personal information.

  1. What must keep working?
  2. What could fail, including shared dependencies?
  3. Who has authority and who substitutes?
  4. What is the minimum service and tolerable outage?
  5. What alternatives are independently tested?
  6. What triggers switch, safe stop or mutual aid?
  7. How are records reconciled and service restored?
  8. Who tests the plan, when, and with what evidence?
