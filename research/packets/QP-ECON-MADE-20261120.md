## 1. Thesis, scope and evidence distinction
Nicki Hart's November 20, 2026 Hart Beat of the City article, "Pueblo County's Economy Survives on Making Things, and the Proof Is Growing", argues that production and external sales drive Pueblo's primary-job economy and reviews four projects: Little Leaf Farms, Ecoasis AgTech Park, Project Falcon and Driven Plastics. This is an attributed argument, not Q Pueblo's endorsed theory or a claim that healthcare, retail, government, education or services have no economic value. Source: https://hartbeatofthecity.substack.com/p/pueblo-countys-economy-survives-on-making-things

The operating test is not announced project value, but decisions documented, financing completed, production actually commenced, payroll jobs delivered, supply-chain demand realized, public costs and measurable net benefits. Each initiative has its own entity/parcel/legal jurisdiction and event ledger; it must not be collapsed into one combined agreement. Company names in the source are attributed leads, not a verified list of current operating sites.

## 2. Verified baseline and limitations
The BLS October 2026 area economic summary reported approximately 5.0 thousand manufacturing payroll jobs in the Pueblo area for August 2026, approximately unchanged year-over-year at the published precision; see https://www.bls.gov/regions/mountain-plains/summary/blssummary_pueblo.pdf and https://www.bls.gov/eag/eag.co_pueblo_msa.htm. This is an area-level estimate, not a verified measure of jobs associated with the four developments. The article reports 6.1% unemployment, but its month, seasonal adjustment and geography are not documented in the article; match an exact BLS LAUS row before adopting the figure.

PEDCO defines primary jobs through products or services primarily sold beyond Pueblo. Primary-job logic is not identical to regional GDP contribution, tax receipts or welfare gains. Source: https://www.pedco.org/how-pedco-grows-pueblos-economy/ . BEA county GDP measures separate market value added and is not a count of project announcements: https://www.bea.gov/data/gdp/gdp-by-county .

## 3. Project-by-project claim and outcome matrix

### Little Leaf Farms
- **Article's projection:** First-phase greenhouse about $150 million, possible second phase around $100 million; over 200 direct primary jobs at wages approaching $59,000 on average; up to $169,600 for training with Pueblo Community College. Groundbreaking targeted January 2027; later phases within five years.
- **Dated government action:** Employment and lease items moved toward November 23, 2026 Pueblo City Council deliberation; see the article's linked draft agreements and https://civiciq.com/public-project/the-council-scheduled-an-ordinance-approving-an-employment-b0a976cd-88af-56f9-b351-2cbdad43090d .
- **Stage:** Proposal / scheduled decision, not final vote, completed construction or payroll.
- **Missing controlling evidence:** final meeting votes, executed employment and lease contracts, project ID, incentives by stage, parcels and water agreements, construction financing, phased hiring milestones, training receipts, crop production/offtake.

### Ecoasis AgTech Park
- **Developer/reporting projection:** 272 acres of campus with approximately 160 acres of greenhouses, around 1,000 jobs at full build-out, food crops and limited-footprint data center utilizing recovered heat.
- **Independent public reporting:** KRDO November 16 reporting describes community questions around the data center and county approval; https://krdo.com/news/2026/11/16/greenhouse-development-could-bring-jobs-south-of-pueblo-people-still-unsure-because-of-included-data-center/ . Industry report: https://www.hortidaily.com/article/9894962/160-acre-colorado-greenhouse-park-to-run-on-waste-heat-from-a-co-located-data-center/ .
- **Stage:** Proposed; county land-use and utility evidence not established.
- **Material uncertainties:** ownership/developer, parcel and jurisdiction; PUD hearing records; cooling architecture; water rights and actual acre-feet/year; heat exchanger efficiency; electricity MW/MWh and grid upgrade expense; construction vs direct permanent full-time employment; financed vs conceptual phases.
- **History preserved:** Existing 2026-11-10 candidate packet at research/packets/QP-ECON-2026-ECOASIS-001.md remains a historical snapshot; new source information is an amendment, not a replacement. Do not merge Ecoasis with Project Deep Root absent entity/parcel proof.

### Project Falcon
- **Reporting:** Proposed $800 million multiphase advanced tissue manufacturing development with about 293 jobs and roughly $80,000 average wages. Source https://www.koaa.com/news/local-news/in-your-community/pueblo/pueblo-mayor-provides-update-on-secretive-project-falcon .
- **Event history:** June negotiation pause and council deferral; reported October 26 approval of a land option, distinct from sale, factory approval or money paid; new MOU described in the November 20 article as pending November 23.
- **Stage:** Option/negotiation, with decision pending; not verified operating.
- **Public-money controls:** Do not combine early $65 million discussion, draft $15.2 million cash incentive or proposed land/infrastructure/training items as one currently approved expenditure. Obtain current version of MOU, votes, City legal constraints, infrastructure agreements, use of Half-Cent Funds and clawbacks.
- **Continuity:** Link public/project-falcon.html for prior chronology and public/report-lime-road-costs.html for shared infrastructure allocation.

### Driven Plastics
- **Observed rather than forecast:** Media and local accounts document recycled-polymer asphalt manufacturing and road use in Pueblo; Medal of Honor Boulevard reportedly opened April 10, 2026. Sources: https://krdo.com/news/top-stories/2026/04/10/pueblo-countys-new-medal-of-honor-boulevard-officially-opens-today/ and https://coloradosun.com/2024/07/15/colorado-recycling-companies/ . Siloam Road was an earlier demonstration.
- **Article's reported financial history:** 2021 17-job commitment, $375,000 Half-Cent incentive, $500,000 state Advanced Industries Accelerator award and another state grant in 2026. These dollar amounts require individual award/agreement and actual disbursement checks before Q Pueblo can call them paid.
- **Stage:** Existing production/application demonstrated; long-term performance and net external sales not independently measured.
- **Independent outcome design:** track material source and lifecycle, road segment length, contract amount, equivalent conventional pavement baseline, wear/cracking/potholes, maintenance events, temperature exposure, potential microplastics release and disposal.

## 5. Cross-system impact analysis
**Workforce:** compare wage distribution, benefits, local hire, shift schedules, accessible transportation, apprenticeships and training completion—not only average announced wages.  
**Water:** quantify withdrawals, net consumption, peak demand, decreed rights and drought resilience separately; greenhouses and data centers have different regimes.  
**Power:** state nameplate MW, annual MWh, coincident peak and backup emissions; evaluate industrial tariffs, grid upgrades and other customers' exposure.  
**Land and infrastructure:** parcel title, roads, water mains, stormwater, wastewater, flood risk and maintenance obligations.  
**Local enterprise and households:** supplier contracts, actual procurement, fiscal diversification, small-business opportunities, housing and school impacts.  
**Risk:** announcement-to-build failure, concentration in one employer, economic-cycle sensitivity, automation, cost overruns, environmental impacts, changing federal/state incentives and public cost before private investment.
